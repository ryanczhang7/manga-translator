## Context

**Filed 2026-09-20 by the Lead PO, out of a measurement dossier the orchestrator
gathered at PLANNED against this worktree at `b7250eb`.** Last of four —
[[MT-039]] (the floor), [[MT-040]] (the manifest parser), [[MT-041]] (the guard
hot path), **MT-042** (this). [[MT-039]] `## Notes` PO-2 records why the work
splits this way.

The other three make the self-test **cheaper**. This one makes it **wider**:
`selftest.sh` runs its twelve suites strictly serially, and a GitHub job is
billed on its own wall-clock, so running them concurrently reduces **billed
minutes** as well as elapsed time. (That is unlike a matrix, which shards
wall-clock while *increasing* billed minutes.) The runner has 4 vCPUs and the
work is spawn-bound.

### The risk, which is different in kind from the other three stories

[[MT-040]] and [[MT-041]] change **how work is done** and are held to
byte-identical output and unchanged verdicts. This story changes **the runner
that decides whether the harness is trustworthy at all**, and its failure modes
are the classic ones of shell concurrency: a background job's exit status
dropped, output from two suites interleaved into unreadable mush, and a fixture
collision that makes a suite fail once a month for reasons nobody can
reproduce. `selftest.sh` is what CI runs *first*, ahead of the gates, precisely
because everything else depends on it.

### Parallel-safety, surveyed and then reproduced independently

The dossier surveyed it. Reproduced at PLANNED by the Lead PO, by grepping
every suite for writes outside its own fixture:

```
$ grep -n "claude/state\|REPO_ROOT" .claude/tests/*.test.sh
```

Every `.claude/state/**` path in every suite is under `"$FIX"` — the per-suite
`mktemp -d` fixture — never under the real repository. Every `$REPO_ROOT`
reference is a **read**: `ci-local.test.sh:16,17` (the script and the workflow
dir), `lib.test.sh:14,352` (sourcing the library, listing shipped scripts),
`gates.test.sh:742` (reading the shipped `project.conf`),
`gate-reminder.test.sh:49` (invoking the real hook with `CLAUDE_PROJECT_DIR`
pointed at the fixture), `new-story.test.sh:22` (the script).
`[MEASURED — 2026-09-20, Lead PO, this worktree at b7250eb]`

So the survey holds: suites are write-isolated. **Two things it does not
settle**, and AC-5 is about them:

1. **`mutate.test.sh:122`** runs
   `mutate src/main.ts 's/90/-90/' -- sh -c 'rm -f .claude/state/mutations/*.bak'`
   — a **relative** path, resolved against whatever the working directory is at
   that moment. Under concurrency, a suite that `cd`s is a suite that can reach
   out of its fixture. Verify, do not assume.
2. **Shared *read* of a file another suite might write.** No suite writes
   `project.conf` or `paths.conf` today, but [[MT-034]] DV-3 records a probe
   that appended twin rules to the real `paths.conf` and restored it with `cp`.
   A future probe doing that while `profiles.test.sh` reads the same file is a
   flake that appears once and never reproduces. Worth an assertion or an
   explicit note.

Nothing outside `.claude/tests/` parses selftest stdout — `ci-local.sh:72` only
runs it and reads its exit code — so per-suite output buffering has no
downstream consumer.

### Two facts about this story's own shape, and both are traps

**1. The phase lock will enforce nothing here.** `scripts/selftest.sh` and
`.claude/tests/*.test.sh` both classify `harness`, which every phase permits
(measured in [[MT-039]] and [[MT-041]]). **RED→GREEN is honoured by the story,
not enforced by the hook.** Mutation probes go through
`bash scripts/mutate.sh`, never `sed -i`.

**2. No gate judges the harness.** The `story-authoring` skill says to name the
required gate that would fail if this story's artifact broke. **There is
none** — all eight gates are `uv run` against `src/mangatl`. `required_gates:
[]` is a decision. The judge is the `Harness self-test` CI step plus
`scripts/ci-local.sh`. **Do not add a `selftest` gate to `project.conf`**: CI
would run the self-test twice, which in this story is not merely wasteful but
self-defeating.

## Acceptance criteria

<!-- AC-1 is the cost claim. AC-2 is the control that catches the classic bug
     and is the single most important criterion here. AC-3, AC-4 and AC-5 are
     the interface, the floor and the isolation. Note that AC-2 and AC-5 are
     what make AC-1 worth having: a concurrent runner that loses a failure is
     strictly worse than a slow serial one. -->

- **AC-1** — Given all twelve suites and a machine with at least 4 CPUs, when
  `bash scripts/selftest.sh` runs, then its wall-clock is **at most 60%** of the
  serial wall-clock measured on the same machine in the same session,
  back to back, with nothing else running.
  *Baseline to be re-measured before RED (see `## Context`); the PLANNED-time
  serial figure was 2,293 s on this machine, contended.* **60% is the weakest
  target worth the risk** — below that, the story does not earn its failure
  modes and should have been closed at the re-measure gate.
  *Control:* AC-2, AC-4 and AC-5. Measuring only AC-1 would reward a runner
  that starts twelve suites and waits for none of them.

- **AC-2** — *(negative control for AC-1, and the classic bug)* Given a run in
  which suite *k* **fails** and every suite after it passes, when the self-test
  runs, then the run **exits non-zero**, the failing suite's output is shown in
  full, and the trailing line reports the count in the shipped format
  `%d of %d harness suite(s) FAILED.`
  *Measured today: the serial runner increments `fails` on a non-zero `bash
  "$suite"` and prints exactly that line.* A concurrent rewrite that reads
  `$?` of the wait rather than of each job, or that pipes a suite into anything
  without `set -o pipefail`, **exits 0 here** — which is a self-test that
  reports success over a failing harness, i.e. strictly worse than the slow
  runner it replaced.
  *The failing suite must not be the last one*, because a runner that only ever
  reports the final job's status passes that case by accident.

- **AC-3** — Given the existing interface, when it is exercised after this
  change, then all of it still works: `bash scripts/selftest.sh phase-guard`
  runs **exactly one** suite; `bash scripts/selftest.sh nosuchsuite` prints
  `No suites matched 'nosuchsuite'. Looked in .claude/tests/*.test.sh` and
  **exits 1**; `VERBOSE=1 bash scripts/selftest.sh <suite>` still names every
  assertion; and a successful full run prints
  `%d harness suite(s) passed.` and **exits 0**.
  *All measured today from `scripts/selftest.sh` as shipped.*

- **AC-4** — Given concurrent execution, when the run completes, then each
  suite's output appears **contiguously** — no line of one suite interleaved
  into another — each preceded by its `\n=== <name> ===\n` header, and the
  suites appear in the **same deterministic order as today** (the shell glob's
  order over `.claude/tests/*.test.sh`), regardless of the order in which they
  finished.
  *Measured today: serial execution gives both properties for free.*
  *Control:* a run in which the shortest suite (`new-story`, 2.5 s) and the
  longest (`phase-guard`, ~22 min) are both scheduled must still print
  `new-story` before `phase-guard` if the glob does. A runner that prints in
  completion order fails this, and would make every future diff of self-test
  output unreadable.

- **AC-5** — *(the isolation control)* Given the twelve suites run
  concurrently, when the full run is repeated **three times**, then all twelve
  pass every time, and the real repository is **unmodified** after each run:
  `git status --porcelain` is empty and `.claude/state/` contains no file the
  run created outside a fixture.
  *Measured today: every `.claude/state/**` write in every suite is under its
  own `mktemp -d` fixture, and every `$REPO_ROOT` reference is a read
  (`## Context`).* Three repetitions rather than one because a fixture
  collision is a **race**, and a race that appears one run in three is
  indistinguishable from a pass if you run once.
  *Named control:* `mutate.test.sh:122` uses a relative path
  (`.claude/state/mutations/*.bak`). Assert explicitly that running `mutate`
  concurrently with `phase-guard` — the two suites that both exercise
  `mutate.sh` and the guard — leaves no `.bak` in the real
  `.claude/state/mutations/`. A stray `.bak` there means a restore escaped its
  fixture, and `rules.md` says a leftover `.bak` is how a failed restore
  announces itself.

- **AC-6** — Given [[MT-039]]'s per-suite floors, when the concurrent run
  completes, then **every suite's executed assertion count is read and checked
  exactly as in the serial runner**, and no floor is unmet.
  *This is stated as its own criterion because output capture is the mechanism
  MT-039 uses to read `summary()`, and a concurrency rewrite re-plumbs exactly
  that.* The obvious way to break it is to buffer per suite for AC-4 and read
  the count from the wrong buffer, or to skip the check for a suite that
  finished while another was being drained.

### C-2 The mechanism

Bash job control plus per-suite output buffering. Constraints, each of which is
a way this is got wrong:

- **Bounded parallelism**, not twelve at once. `N = 4` or `$(nproc)` where
  available, defaulting to 4 — the runner's vCPU count. Unbounded jobs on a
  spawn-bound workload on Windows makes things slower, not faster.
- **Collect each job's exit status individually.** `wait "$pid"` returns that
  job's status; a bare `wait` does not. AC-2 is this constraint.
- **Buffer to a file per suite, print in glob order after each completes in
  turn.** Printing as jobs finish gives completion order and fails AC-4.
- **The buffer files are temporary and must be named explicitly.** `rules.md`
  and `lib.test.sh:362` forbid a shipped script depending on `$TMPDIR` or
  `mktemp`: *"no shipped script depends on $TMPDIR or mktemp"* is an existing
  assertion, because `$TMPDIR` is unset in some of the shells this harness runs
  in and a backup once went nowhere because of it. **Buffer under
  `.claude/state/`** (which `.gitignore` covers and which
  `.claude/state/README.md` documents — a new state file must be added to that
  table with a commit yes/no, or `.claude/tests/settings.test.sh` fails).
- **Clean up the buffers**, and leave them behind on failure only if that is
  deliberate and documented.
- **Bash 3.2 compatible**, no `wait -n`, which is bash 4.3. Bash, awk and
  coreutils only; **no python** (`rules.md`, Portability).
- `VERBOSE=1` must reach each suite (it is an exported env var, so it does —
  confirm it).

### C-3 An escape hatch, decided in advance rather than improvised

If a suite proves not to be parallel-safe, the runner must be able to pin it
serially rather than the story having to edit the suite. Decide the mechanism
at RED and pin it here: a list in `selftest.sh`, or a marker line in the suite
file. **Prefer the list in `selftest.sh`**, because it is visible in one place
and because a marker in a suite file makes the suite's own content load-bearing
for the runner. Whichever is chosen, a serially-pinned suite still has its
floor read (AC-6) and still appears in glob order (AC-4).

If no suite needs it, ship the mechanism anyway with zero entries and one
assertion that it works — a hatch discovered to be needed at 2 a.m. is a hatch
that gets improvised.

### C-5 The earning rule

AC-2, AC-3, AC-4 and AC-5 are all written against a runner that **already
satisfies them serially**, so they pass on their first execution and per
`rules.md` are not yet tests. **Earn them before the rewrite lands**, through
`bash scripts/mutate.sh`, never `sed -i`. Three probes:

1. **AC-2's.** Mutate the serial runner's failure accounting — `s/fails=$((fails+1))/:/`
   — and watch AC-2's assertion go red with the runner exiting 0 over a failing
   suite. This is the assertion that must discriminate, because AC-2 is the
   whole safety case for the story.
2. **AC-3's.** Mutate the `[ -n "$ONLY" ] && [ "$ONLY" != "$name" ] && continue`
   filter so single-suite selection runs everything, and watch AC-3 go red.
3. **AC-4's.** Mutate the loop to iterate the glob in reverse and watch AC-4's
   ordering assertion go red. If it does not, the assertion is not checking
   order.

Paste each mutation, the red output, and `mutate.sh`'s `restored (verified)`
line into `## Test plan` (RED) or `## Regressions`.

**And note the asymmetry:** these three earn the assertions against the
**serial** runner. GATES must re-run equivalents against the **concurrent** one
(DV-2), because the concurrent runner's failure modes are not the serial one's.

## Out of scope

**Every item below is a non-goal. Touching one is a reason to stop and raise
it, not a free win.**

1. **Any change to `.github/workflows/**` or `scripts/ci-local.sh`.**
   `.claude/tests/ci-local.test.sh` derives `ci-local.sh`'s expected step list
   from the `run:` lines of `gates.yml` and `boundaries.yml` **in both
   directions**, so a step added on one side and not the other fails the
   self-test. If this story ever needs a workflow step changed, the matching
   `ci-local.sh` change lands in the same commit and that test must pass both
   ways. It should not need to: the workflow calls `bash scripts/selftest.sh`
   and will keep calling exactly that.
   **And note the sharp edge** ([[MT-039]] PO-1 reason 4): that test greps
   `run:` lines **only**, so a step made conditional with `if:` or `paths:`
   keeps its `run:` line and the test keeps passing while local and CI stop
   meaning the same thing. Nothing in this story should go near it.
2. **A GitHub Actions matrix.** It shards wall-clock while *increasing* billed
   minutes, which is the opposite of this story's purpose; and it would change
   the workflow, engaging item 1.
3. **Editing any suite to make it parallel-safe.** C-3's escape hatch exists so
   the runner can accommodate a suite rather than the suite accommodating the
   runner. A suite that genuinely needs fixing gets its own story.
4. **Making any individual suite faster.** [[MT-040]] and [[MT-041]] do that,
   and they land first. A change here that also speeds up a suite makes AC-1's
   before/after unreadable.
5. **Conditional execution of the self-test.** Declined in [[MT-039]] PO-1,
   with reasons and with the fallback to adopt if it is ever revisited.
6. **Fixture setup cost** (dossier R-5: `make_project_fixture` does `cp -r` ×2
   plus `git init`/`add`/`commit` per call). Unmeasured; see [[MT-039]] out of
   scope 8. It is tempting here because concurrency makes fixture setup a
   larger share of the critical path — which is a reason to **measure** it
   afterwards, in its own story.
7. **Lowering [[MT-039]]'s floors.** AC-6. If a floor is genuinely wrong that is
   an escalation and an `## Amendments` entry on MT-039, never a quiet edit.

## Notes

### PO-1 — Why this story is filed even though it may never be built

Three reasons. First, the measurement that motivates it is real and recorded;
without a story it becomes folklore, and the next person re-derives the
38-minute profile from scratch. Second, the **condition** under which it is not
worth building (DV-0) is itself a decision worth writing down before anyone is
invested in the outcome — deciding it after [[MT-041]] lands, with the work
half-scoped, is how a story gets built because it exists rather than because it
pays. Third, EPIC-01's ledger stays honest either way: a story closed on a
measurement is a closed story with a reason.

### PO-2 — Why this is a separate story from MT-040 and MT-041

It changes the **runner**, not the measured code. Different file, different
failure modes — interleaved output, a lost background exit status, a fixture
race — and, uniquely among the four, a value that cannot be judged until the
other two have landed. Full sizing reasoning in [[MT-039]] `## Notes` PO-2.

### PO-3 — The billed-minutes argument, stated once so it is not re-argued

A GitHub job is billed on its own wall-clock. Running twelve suites inside one
job concurrently therefore reduces **billed minutes** as well as elapsed time.
A matrix — twelve jobs, one suite each — reduces elapsed time but **increases**
billed minutes, because each job is billed separately and each pays its own
checkout and `setup-uv`. On `windows-latest` the multiplier is 2× ([[MT-001]]
PO-3, recorded in `gates.yml`), so the matrix option is worse on cost by more
than it looks. Out of scope 2.
