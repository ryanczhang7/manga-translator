## Context

EPIC-09 PO-3's measurement. MT-107 made the context mode selectable with
`rolling` still the default; this spike measures the two on the held-out
benchmark chapter (`product-brief.md` O1; EPIC-07; `bench.truth`) on Gemini,
with the glossary pre-pass (MT-105) in place for both, and records which the
synchronous run should default to. **It can come out either way**, and the
decision rule is written here, before the numbers exist, so the result is a
measurement and not a preference:

> `neighbours` becomes the synchronous default unless its S1b (chapter
> acceptance, `bench.acceptance`) is more than **2.0 points** below
> `rolling`'s. Cost (S5) is reported and does not decide: both modes send a
> context block of the same cap. (EPIC-09 Q-5: the user may move the bar
> before the runs.)

**The user runs it** (Q-1): two full chapter runs on the user's key (about
$0.11 each by MT-080's measurement, up to ~$0.60 each by `cost-model.awk`
tokens x Gemini rates), one per mode, a review pass of each in the window,
then `python -m mangatl.bench FOLDER` on each. The benchmark chapter must not
have been used in development (`BenchmarkChapterUsedInDevelopment` refuses
otherwise), and the ground truth must validate.

### Who judges a line (PO-1, decided by the user 2026-11-06)

The reviewer does not read Japanese, so "accepted as-is" cannot mean "I
checked it against the Japanese". It means this, applied to every region:

1. **Meaning, against an official English release.** The benchmark chapter is
   one with a published English translation. The reviewer keeps the app's line
   only if it says what the official line for that bubble says - meaning, not
   wording. The official release is a reference for meaning only; it is
   **not** the target text, because official releases are often not good
   English either.
2. **English, by the reviewer's own judgement.** Grammar, readability and
   writing style. A line that matches the official meaning but reads badly is
   edited, exactly as a mistranslation is.

Anything else is an edit, to what the reviewer would publish. Where the app
and the official release disagree in meaning and the reviewer cannot tell
which is right, the reviewer edits to the official meaning (a rejection) and
lists the region under *Meaning disputes* in their notes, so the count of
those rejections that may be the official release's fault is visible.

The metric is unchanged - `norm(proposed) == norm(final)` (stack.md §5/O2) -
only the reviewer's rule for editing is fixed here.

### Running the two modes (PO-2, PO-3)

- **Two copies of the chapter folder.** `mangatl-run` resumes a folder's
  existing project rather than translating again, so each mode runs on its own
  copy, with its own copy of the `.truth.json` beside it.
- **Blind review.** The copies are named neutrally (`<chapter>-A`,
  `<chapter>-B`), the orchestrator assigns the modes and keeps the mapping out
  of sight until both reviews are rendered. Reviewing the same chapter twice
  biases the second pass; not knowing which mode is which keeps that bias off
  the decision. The second review is still the second reading - the doc says so.
- **Notes.** While reviewing, the reviewer writes short notes per edited line
  where the reason is worth keeping (grammar, tone, a name, a meaning dispute).
  They feed AC-4 and AC-5.

The output is `docs/wiki/audits/MT-108-context-mode.md` with `## Method`,
`## Results`, `## Decided`. MT-109 reads `## Decided` and nothing else.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** - `## Method` names the chapter (by its page count and a hash of the
  ground-truth file, not its path), the official English release used for
  meaning (publisher, volume, chapter), the commit each run was made on, the
  exact commands (`MANGATL_CONTEXT_MODE=... mangatl-run FOLDER`, then
  `python -m mangatl.bench FOLDER`), the review rule of PO-1 in full, which
  copy was reviewed first, and states that the glossary pre-pass ran in both,
  with the term count it produced each time.
- **AC-2** - `## Results` carries, for each mode, the bench report's six
  numbers verbatim (S1b, S1a, recall, spurious, S2, S5 total and per page)
  and the ledger's call count; the two S5 totals are within the $2.00 ceiling
  or the document says which page the guard refused. It also carries, per
  mode, the count of *Meaning disputes*.
- **AC-3** - `## Decided` applies the rule above to the two S1b figures,
  states the default MT-109 is to set, and - if the figures differ by less
  than one point - says the measurement does not separate the modes and that
  `neighbours` is chosen for its order independence under the rule as
  written.
- **AC-4** - `## Results` names at least three lines where the two modes
  disagree in acceptance, with both renderings and the official line, so the
  decision has a qualitative reading beside the number. *Control:* a
  rolling-mode line whose acceptance depended on the previous page's English
  (a continued sentence across a page break) is looked for and reported,
  found or not.
- **AC-5** - `## Results` summarises the reviewer's notes on English quality
  per mode - the recurring kinds of edit (grammar, tone, register, names,
  literalness) with a count or an example each - and says whether either
  mode's prose read better end to end, in the reviewer's words. It is a
  reading, not a score, and does not enter the rule.
