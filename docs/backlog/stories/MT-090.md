## Context

Filed 2026-10-21 by the Lead PO from MT-086 GREEN, by the user's decision (a
follow-up story rather than a scope change to MT-086). After MT-086 the 28 px
rail (`STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH`, `layout.md` §"The one breakpoint") shows each page's
status glyph above its 1-based index. The feature-developer measured the rail
row's `sizeHint().width()` at 13 / 26 / **39 px** for indices 1 / 10 / 100
(offscreen platform, fallback font), so a chapter of 100 or more pages
overflows the rail from page 100 on. No MT-086 criterion covers it: its tests
use chapters of at most 8 pages.

**Open design question for PLANNED (Lead Designer):** smaller numerals for
three digits, a wider rail when the chapter has ≥ 100 pages, or the index
dropped in favour of the glyph alone with the number in the tooltip and the
accessible name (which already carries it). Manga chapters are usually under
60 pages; volumes run past 100. Gate that fails if it breaks: `unit` /
`coverage` (`covers src/mangatl/**`).

Pinned 2026-10-23 at PLANNED → RED from the Lead Designer's decision in
`## Design notes` (per-row fit; the rail stays 28 px; the tooltip carries the
number a row cannot draw). The draft AC-1 ("a way to read its page number") is
sharpened here, while the story is still at PLANNED, to name that way exactly.

- **AC-1 — every rail row fits and stays readable.** Given a chapter of 120
  pages (filenames `p001.png` … `p120.png`) in a window 1100 px wide, when the
  strip is laid out as the rail, then for every row N (1-based): the row
  widget's `sizeHint().width()` is at most `STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH`; its `pageGlyph`
  is visible; every rail row has the same `sizeHint().height()` as every other;
  and **either** its `pageLabel` is visible with text `str(N)` **or** the
  item's tooltip is exactly `f"Page {N} of 120 — {filename}"` (spaced em
  dash, the whole string) — never neither, never a tooltip that merely contains
  the number. The item's `text()` is `str(N)` and its accessible name is
  `page_accessible_text(...)`'s unchanged `"Page N of 120, filename, phrase"`
  for every row.
- **AC-2 — the split lands exactly where the font puts it.** Given the same
  chapter and width, and given that a `QLabel` showing `"100"` in the rail
  label's font has `sizeHint().width() > STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH` while one showing
  `"99"` does not (the suite's offscreen font: 36 px and 24 px; the test asserts
  this premise first, so a font change fails it loudly), then rows 1–99 show
  their `pageLabel` and carry the filename as tooltip, and rows 100–120 hide
  their `pageLabel` (`isVisibleTo(strip)` is False) and carry the page tooltip
  of AC-1. A chapter of 7 pages at 1100 px is unchanged: every label visible,
  every tooltip the filename.
- **AC-3 — the full strip and the crossing are untouched.** Given the 120-page
  chapter at 1900 px, then every row's `pageLabel` is visible with `str(N)` and
  every tooltip is the filename. When the window is resized 1900 → 1100 → 1900,
  rows 100–120 go shown → hidden → shown, every tooltip follows (filename →
  page tooltip → filename), and `item.text()` stays `str(N)` throughout.

**Block 1 — the tooltip text (mechanical).** `mangatl.ui.workspace` exports

    def page_tooltip_text(index: int, total_pages: int, filename: str) -> str

`index` is 0-based like `page_accessible_text`; the result numbers pages from
one: `page_tooltip_text(99, 120, "p100.png") == "Page 100 of 120 — p100.png"`
(U+2014 EM DASH with one space either side; voice.md's own dash form). It is
the whole tooltip of a rail row whose index is hidden; the filename alone stays
the tooltip of every other row and of every full-strip row.

**Block 2 — the fit rule (mechanical).** On `_PageRow` (private, found as
`strip.itemWidget(strip.item(i))`; its labels by `findChild(QLabel,
"pageGlyph")` / `findChild(QLabel, "pageLabel")`):

    def index_fits(self) -> bool   # self.label.sizeHint().width() <= STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH

The semantics behind the number: the rail row has zero contents margins in
rail mode (`set_rail`), so the label's own hint is the whole horizontal cost
and 28 is the budget; `<=` because a 28 px label fills a 28 px rail exactly.
The rule is judged per row, and re-judged on every `set_rail(True)`, on every
`_relabel`, and on `QEvent.FontChange` for the row (a `changeEvent` override).
It is not judged in the full strip: there the label is always shown.

**Block 3 — what "hidden" means (mechanical).** A rail row whose index does
not fit has `pageLabel.isVisibleTo(strip) is False`, keeps `pageLabel.text()
== str(N)`, keeps `pageGlyph` visible, and its item carries Block 1's tooltip.
Every rail row — hidden or shown — reports the same `sizeHint().height()`, and
every rail row's `sizeHint().width() <= STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH`. The Lead Designer
names `QSizePolicy.setRetainSizeWhenHidden(True)` as the way to keep the
height; note that retaining the size would also retain the 36 px *width* in
the row's hint, so GREEN must satisfy both the height and the width condition
and may choose another mechanism (a minimum height on the glyph/label box, a
retained-size label with an `Ignored` horizontal policy, …). The two
observables are the contract; the mechanism is GREEN's.

**Block 4 — the strip wires it (mechanical).** `PageStrip.set_rail(rail)`,
`PageStrip.set_pages(...)` → `_relabel()` and the row's `FontChange` each
re-judge every row and reset every item's tooltip (filename or Block 1) and
size hint (`_resize_items`). Leaving the rail restores every label and every
filename tooltip. `item.text()`, `AccessibleTextRole` and
`page_accessible_text` are untouched by this story.

**Block 5 — oracle partition.** All three criteria are *mechanical*: the
numbers are read out, not calibrated. Settled baselines (measured 2026-10-23,
`QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen`, app font "Sans Serif 9", no stylesheet): a rail
`pageLabel`'s `sizeHint().width()` is 12 / 24 / 36 for "1" / "10" / "100";
MT-086 measured the whole row at 13 / 26 / 39. AC-2's premise assertion reads
the font the suite actually has rather than assuming these.

## Design notes

Lead Designer, 2026-10-23 (dispatched on `fable`, no override). Settles the
open question in `## Context`.

**Decision — the index is drawn when it fits, hidden when it does not; the
rail stays 28 px; the tooltip carries what the row does not draw.** A hybrid of
option (c), judged **per row**, not per chapter.

Why not the other two:

- *(a) smaller numerals.* Under the suite's font (offscreen fallback, 12 px per
  digit) "100" is 36 px and no legible size reaches 28; `type.caption` is
  already the floor of the type scale (tokens.md §8) and the rail would need
  something below it. A rule that only works on one font is not a design.
- *(b) a wider rail for ≥ 100 pages.* Every pixel comes out of the canvas
  (layout.md "The canvas always wins"), the rail width would depend on the
  chapter opened, and `STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH == 28` is pinned by two frozen tests
  and by A-05 (28 px is the target minimum, not a choice to revisit here).
- *(c) chapter-wide, glyph only.* A 120-page volume would show 120 bare glyphs
  and hide 99 numbers that fit, to accommodate 21 that do not. Page 5 of a
  120-page chapter should still read "5". And in production (Consolas or
  Segoe UI, ~7 px per digit) "120" is ~21 px and fits, so a chapter-wide rule
  would hide everything on one font and nothing on another.

The per-row rule degrades exactly as far as the font forces it and no further.
It keeps A-10's promise — nothing at the narrow width becomes unreachable: the
page number is still in the accessible name ("Page N of M, …", unchanged) and,
for a row that cannot draw it, in the tooltip.

### The rule, mechanically

- **Scope.** Rail only (`PageStrip.is_rail`). In the 96 px strip the index is
  always drawn and the tooltip is always the filename — nothing changes there.
- **Fit test, per row:** the index is drawn iff
  `label.sizeHint().width() <= STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH`. The rail row's contents
  margins are 0 (`_PageRow.set_rail`), so there is no margin term; if a margin
  is ever added, it joins the left-hand side. Re-judged whenever the row enters
  the rail, its text is set (`_relabel`), or its font changes
  (`QEvent.FontChange` — A-14 scaling changes the answer).
- **Drawn (fits):** `pageLabel` visible, text `str(N)`; row tooltip is the
  filename, as today.
- **Hidden (does not fit):** `pageLabel` hidden **with its size retained**
  (`QSizePolicy.setRetainSizeWhenHidden(True)`) so every rail row keeps the
  same height — a list whose rows change height at page 100 breaks scroll
  rhythm; its text stays `str(N)` so crossing to the wide strip shows it with
  no relabel. The row tooltip becomes **`Page N of M — filename`** (spaced em
  dash, voice.md's own form, e.g. "Page 100 of 120 — p100.png"). The glyph is
  drawn in every case.
- **Unchanged in every case:** `item.text() == str(N)`, the accessible name
  from `page_accessible_text` (already "Page N of M, filename, phrase"), the
  glyph and its colour, `STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH == 28`, the row's focus / current /
  hover states.
- **Tokens:** none new. The index keeps the app font today (`theme.qss.in`
  ~l.437: `type.numeric` is not yet a QSS rule on `pageLabel`); when that rule
  lands the same fit test simply returns True more often.

### What the user sees — 120 pages, window 1100 px, rail

| Rows | Offscreen / wide-digit font | Production (Consolas, Segoe UI 9 pt) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | glyph over "N"; tooltip filename | same |
| 10–99 | glyph over "NN"; tooltip filename | same |
| 100–120 | glyph alone, row the same height; tooltip "Page N of 120 — filename" | glyph over "NNN" (~21 px fits); tooltip filename |

Screen reader: identical at every row and every width — "Page 100 of 120,
p100.png, 4 of 6 lines reviewed". Keyboard: Up/Down still traverse every row;
no row is skipped or shortened. Hover: a row whose number is hidden says its
number first, then its filename.

**Chapters under 100 pages are unchanged** on every supported font (two digits
are at most 24 px under the widest font measured), so the frozen 7-page tests
(`test_page_strip_status.py` ~446–480, `test_workspace_breakpoint.py:284`
tooltip == filename) keep passing untouched.

### What the tests should assert (for RED)

1. **Invariant, every font:** for every rail row, `sizeHint().width() <=
   STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH`, `pageGlyph` visible, and either `pageLabel` visible with
   text `str(N)` or the item tooltip is exactly `f"Page {N} of {M} — {name}"`.
   All rail rows share one `sizeHint().height()`.
2. **The split, under the suite's font:** rows 1–99 drawn with tooltip ==
   filename; rows 100–120 hidden with the page tooltip. Assert the premise
   first — a `QLabel` showing "100" in the row's font has `sizeHint().width()
   > STRIP_RAIL_WIDTH` — so a font change fails this test loudly instead of
   letting it pass for the wrong reason.
3. **Full strip at 1900 px, 120 pages:** all 120 labels visible, all tooltips
   the filename.
4. **Crossing:** 1100 → 1900 → 1100 round-trips rows 100+ hidden → shown →
   hidden with `item.text()` constant.
5. Negative controls for the defect-put-back mutation: "always draw" must fail
   (1) on width; "always hide in the rail" must fail the frozen 7-page tests
   and (2). Neither should be satisfied by a tooltip that merely *contains*
   the number — match the whole string (rules.md, needles).

Recorded in `layout.md` §"The one breakpoint" (table row and dated note) and
`components.md` §`PageStrip` (tooltip sentence), both marked "Amended by
MT-090".
