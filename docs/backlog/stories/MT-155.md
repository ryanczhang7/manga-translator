## Context

EPIC-13 priority 5, and **conditional** (epic PO-6): it runs only if regions
still share a balloon after MT-145. They do. The re-count on the MT-143
development set is 3 pairs whose interiors are the same balloon (`## Notes`,
PO-1), and with them the bake still sets one region's English on top of the
other's (`pipeline/bake.py` `_set_blocks`: each region is fitted into the whole
interior `bubble_interior` finds for it).

**What the user decided, 2026-11-19 (`## Notes`, PO-2):** the regions are
**not** joined into one block. Two of the three pairs are two-lobed balloons
with a separate utterance in each lobe, which the user decided on 2026-11-11
stay separate regions (EPIC-13 PO-1, amended). So each region sharing a
balloon is set **in the part of the balloon nearest its own polygon**.

**Which regions share** (PO-9, the Lead PO's decision the user delegated):
region B shares region A's balloon when the centre of B's polygon lies inside
A's interior. That covers the three counted pairs and the pairs where one
region's interior is the whole joined shape and the other's is one lobe of it
(p063 4/5, p066 6/7, p098 0/1).

Required gates: `unit`, `coverage`, `coverage-core` (`typeset/` is held at
100 % by `coverage-core`).

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** - Given a cleaned page with one closed two-lobed balloon and two
  included regions, one in each lobe, each with text to set, when the page is
  baked, then **two** blocks are set, one per region, and each region's
  English is drawn wholly in its own part of the balloon: no glyph box of
  either block crosses to the other region's side of the line midway between
  the two polygons.
- **AC-2** - For the same pair, no glyph box of one block intersects a glyph
  box of the other, and where each text fits its own part the Render dialog
  reports neither line as overflowing.
  *Control (the defect this story removes):* the same two texts, each fitted
  into the whole balloon as today, have at least one pair of intersecting
  glyph boxes. The test asserts this on its own fixture; a fixture on which
  today's blocks do not collide cannot show this criterion.
- **AC-3** - *(control: nothing shared)* Given two regions where neither
  polygon's centre lies in the other's interior, then each is set exactly as
  today, in the interior `bubble_interior` finds for it. This holds for two
  regions in two separate balloons, and for two regions whose interiors
  overlap without either holding the other's centre (a caption box straddling
  a balloon's outline).
- **AC-4** - An excluded region, and a region with no text to set (no line, a
  `failed` line, or blank text), takes no share of a balloon: the other region
  in it is set in the whole interior, exactly as when it is alone on the page.
- **AC-5** - The review screen and the store are unchanged. The bake only
  reads: after a bake and after a Render-dialog preview of a page with a
  shared balloon, the page's regions and lines read back exactly as before.
  Two lines stay two lines.
- **AC-6** - *(one-way sharing)* Given region A whose interior holds the
  centre of region B's polygon while B's interior does not hold A's (a caption
  box inside a balloon), then A is set in the part of its interior nearer its
  own polygon, no glyph box of A's block intersects one of B's, and B is set
  exactly as today.

### C-1 - the rule: `shared_parts`

A new public function in `src/mangatl/typeset/interior.py`, added to
`__all__`, and described as a rule of its own in the module docstring:

```python
def shared_parts(
    interiors: Sequence[Ring],
    polygons: Sequence[Sequence[tuple[int, int]]],
    shape: tuple[int, int],
) -> list[Ring]:
```

`interiors[k]` is the ring `bubble_interior` returned for the region whose
polygon is `polygons[k]`; `shape` is the page's `(H, W)`. The two sequences
are the same length (`zip(..., strict=True)`), every polygon lies on the page,
and the result has one ring per input, in the same order.

1. **The centre** of a polygon is the centre of its bounding box in whole
   pixels: `((min_x + max_x) // 2, (min_y + max_y) // 2)`. It is the point
   `bubble_interior` casts its star from, give or take the half pixel.
2. **Rivals.** `j` is a rival of `i` (`j != i`) when the centre of
   `polygons[j]` is a set pixel of `interiors[i]`'s fill (`_fill`, the
   module's own `cv2.fillPoly`). One-way: `j` can be a rival of `i` without
   `i` being a rival of `j`. Kind is not consulted; a `box` region's interior
   is its polygon, so it has a rival only when another centre lies in its own
   polygon.
3. **No rival: `interiors[i]` is returned unchanged** (the same ring, equal
   under `==`). A page where nothing is shared gets back exactly what went in,
   and no distance is computed for it.
4. **With rivals, the part.** For a polygon `k`, `d_k` is each pixel's
   Euclidean distance to the nearest pixel of `polygons[k]`'s fill:
   `cv2.distanceTransform` of a `uint8` image that is 0 on the fill and 1
   elsewhere, with `cv2.DIST_L2` and `cv2.DIST_MASK_PRECISE` (exact, not the
   3 x 3 or 5 x 5 approximation). Region `i` **keeps** a pixel of its
   interior's fill when, for every rival `j`, `d_i < d_j`, or `d_i == d_j` and
   `i < j` (a tie goes to the earlier region in reading order). The part is
   the kept pixels **united with `polygons[i]`'s fill**.
5. **The ring of a part** is the external contour of the connected piece of
   the part that holds `polygons[i]`'s fill - the piece holding the most of
   that fill's pixels, which is the whole of it whenever the fill is connected,
   as a filled simple polygon is - closed, holes filled, in Python `int`s, as
   every interior is traced. Not the piece of largest area.
   *Amended in RED, 2026-11-20 (R-1): the largest piece can be one without the
   polygon - on the U balloon of `_bubble_world.u_balloon_page` the arm
   region's kept pixels are a 23,851 px piece holding all 19,951 px of its
   polygon and a 33,330 px piece holding none, and `_outer_ring` traced the
   second, breaking "the floor is the polygon".*

**Why this rule and not another** (PO-2, PO-9). "The part of the balloon
nearest its own polygon" is, read literally, the Voronoi cell of the polygon
inside the interior; the distance transform computes exactly that and needs no
constant. A cut at the neck was rejected: it needs the neck found (convexity
defects, a depth threshold), and p082's balloon has no neck at all. A split of
the *union* of the two interiors was rejected: with one-way sharing the
contained region must keep its own lobe untouched (AC-6), so each region is
cut out of **its own** interior. Where two interiors are the same balloon the
two readings differ by a handful of pixels.

**Why the centre and not "the interiors overlap" or "IoU >= 0.9"** is PO-9,
with the measurements. The rule has **no tunable constant**; nobody adds one.

**What a part is when it is not a ring, and the floor.**

- The kept pixels can fall into several pieces or have a hole. Step 5 takes
  the outer contour of the piece that holds the region's polygon fill, holes
  filled; the other pieces are dropped and belong to no part. Of the eight
  development-set parts the PO inspected, one traced from two contours (p066
  region 7); there the largest piece happened to be the one with the polygon.
  *Amended in RED, 2026-11-20 (R-1): was "takes the largest piece's outer
  contour", which on the U balloon traced a piece holding 0 px of the
  region's polygon (C-1 step 5, amendment).*
- **The floor is the polygon.** The union in step 4 means a part always holds
  the region's own polygon fill, so it is never empty, and it keeps the
  property the bake area relies on: an interior is a superset of its polygon.
  Two parts can therefore overlap only where the two polygons themselves do.
  There is no other floor, no minimum area and no fallback to the whole
  interior: a part too small for its text overflows by `typeset`'s own rule
  (14 px, `overflowed`), is clipped to the bake area and is named in the
  Render dialog, as any overflow is today.
- **p082's kind of pair** (PO-1: `君だー` and the detached end of its dash, one
  balloon, no neck). The dash is a region like any other: it gets the part of
  the balloon nearer its own polygon, however few glyphs it holds, and the
  words get the rest. Nothing is sized in proportion to the text, and the two
  are not joined (joining was option A, and merging regions is detection's
  job). Measured on the real pair with placeholder text: the words at 42 px in
  the upper part, the dash at 42 px in the lower one, the two rings disjoint.

### C-2 - `_set_blocks` sets each candidate in its part

- **Candidates** are exactly the regions `_set_blocks` sets today: not
  excluded, `line is not None`, `line.status != "failed"`, and
  `effective_text(line).strip()` non-empty. Only candidates are handed to
  `shared_parts`, in reading order. A region that is not a candidate is never
  a rival and gets no part (AC-4).
- For the candidates: `interiors = [bubble_interior(cleaned, polygon, kind)]`
  as today, then `parts = shared_parts(interiors, polygons, cleaned.shape)`,
  then `typeset(effective_text(line), part, faces)` for each. **Nothing else
  decides a size or a position**: no clamp, no retry, no nudge. Each block is
  centred where `typeset` centres any block, on the bounding-box centre of the
  part's fitting area.
- Each entry is `(reading_index, part, block)`. **A page with no rivals
  returns what it returns today, value for value** (C-1 step 3), so every
  existing bake test passes unchanged.
- `_bake_area` receives the parts where it received the interiors. A part is
  its region's interior minus pixels given to a rival, so the bake area can
  only stay or shrink, and it still holds every included polygon.

### C-6 - oracle partition

Every criterion is **mechanical**: pin it exactly, invent no metric. The one
measurement with a control is AC-2's glyph-box intersection, and its control
is today's behaviour on the same fixture (C-5 gives the counts).

| Criterion | Oracle |
|---|---|
| AC-1, rings | for the two-lobed fixture, `shared_parts` returns two rings whose fills are disjoint and together equal the interior; every pixel of part `i` is no farther from polygon `i` than from the other by more than 1 px, the distances computed in the test from the rectangles' coordinates (not with `cv2.distanceTransform`) |
| AC-1, bake | two entries; every glyph box of the left block has `x1 <= 320` and every one of the right has `x0 >= 320`; equivalently, baked ink that differs from the cleaned page lies on each text's own side |
| AC-2 | no pair of glyph boxes, one from each block, has an intersection of positive area; `preview_bake(...).overflowing == ()`; each block equals `typeset(text, part, faces)` computed in the test. Control: the blocks `typeset` gives each text in the whole interior have at least one intersecting pair |
| AC-3 | `shared_parts` returns its `interiors` argument's rings unchanged for both fixtures; through the bake, each block equals `typeset(text, bubble_interior(...), faces)` |
| AC-4 | with the right-lobe region excluded, then with no line, a `failed` line, and a blank proposal, the left region's block equals `typeset(text, bubble_interior(...), faces)`: the whole balloon |
| AC-5 | the store reads are equal before and after (C-4) |
| AC-6 | A's ring differs from its interior and its fill holds no pixel of B's polygon; B's ring is B's polygon; no intersecting glyph-box pair |

A glyph box is `PlacedGlyph.ink`, `(x0, y0, x1, y1)`, skipped when `None`.
Two boxes intersect when `min(a.x1, b.x1) > max(a.x0, b.x0)` and `min(a.y1,
b.y1) > max(a.y0, b.y0)`.

### C-7 - known limits, recorded so nobody rediscovers them as bugs

- **Two interiors can overlap with neither holding the other's centre**, and
  then nothing is split (AC-3 says so). The PO's first fixture attempt was
  such a pair of stars and its two texts collided. On the development set no
  such pair collides (DV-4), and the wider rule that would catch it costs a
  regression there (PO-9).
- **Three or more regions in one balloon** each keep what is nearer their own
  polygon than every rival's. The rule is stated for any number; the
  development set has no triple and no test is asked for one.
- **A part's ring can be a self-touching contour**, as some interiors already
  are today (measured: p075 regions 7 and 8, p066 region 7, p090 region 5
  before any split). `typeset` takes them as it does today.

### Export shape the tests pin (fact, not suggestion)

- `mangatl.typeset.interior.shared_parts(interiors, polygons, shape)`: called
  positionally with `interiors` a `list[Ring]`, `polygons` a `list[Ring]`,
  `shape` the tuple `(560, 640)`. The result is passed to `list(...)` and
  compared to a `list[Ring]`, and indexed `[0]`, `[1]`: any sequence of rings
  passes; `list[Ring]` as C-1 says. Each ring is compared with `==` to a tuple
  of `(int, int)` tuples, checked closed (`part[0] == part[-1]`) and every
  coordinate `type(v) is int`. Lengths that differ raise `ValueError` (what
  `zip(strict=True)` raises). It is read as a module attribute, so it must be
  a module-level name of `mangatl.typeset.interior` (and in `__all__`, per C-1,
  which no test checks).

  unchanged signature (C-3), called positionally with lists, `load_faces()`,
  and a `(H, W)` `uint8` grey array; the result is compared with `==` to a
  `list` of `(int, Ring, TypesetBlock)` tuples. **It must return a `list`**,
  not another sequence: `[...] == tuple` is false.
