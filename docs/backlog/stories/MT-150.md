## Context

EPIC-13 priority 3. The user decided this on 2026-11-07 (U-3): the bench
gains a check for p017's slip, reported beside S1b and not part of it. In
copy A, the slip left two neighbouring pairs of regions with **identical
English from different Japanese**: regions 4/5 ("WEE" for `っ` and
`いてーよー！！`) and 6/7 ("It hurts!!" for `いてーよー！！` and the battlefield
line). The pattern is cheap to count and needs no truth. It does not see a
slip that produces different English, so it is a floor, and the report says
so.

S1b did not move in MT-108. The reviewer accepted both copies' lines
unedited, so a slip is invisible to the headline (MT-108 "What S1b does and
does not say"). That is why it is its own line.

Required gates: `unit`, `coverage`.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** - Given a page whose regions i and i+1 (reading order, both
  included, both proposed) have `normalise(proposed_en)` equal and non-empty,
  and `normalise(source_ja)` different, when `count_slips(project)` runs,
  then it reports that pair as `(ordinal, i)`.
- **AC-2** - *(control: repeated dialogue is not a slip)* Two neighbours
  with identical Japanese and identical English (copy B's
  `いてーよー！！` → "It huuurts!!" twice) are **not** reported.
- **AC-3** - *(control: neighbours only)* Equal English from different
  Japanese two regions apart, or across a page break, is not reported.
- **AC-4** - Excluded regions and regions with no proposal are skipped. The
  pair is formed over the remaining regions in reading order, so a skipped
  region between two equal neighbours makes them neighbours.
- **AC-5** - `python -m mangatl.bench` prints, after the S1a line,
  `slips (identical English, different Japanese)  N`. When N > 0, the next
  line lists `page P regions i/i+1` for each pair. Neither changes S1b, S1a
  or S2's figures or verdicts.
- **AC-6** - It reads `proposed_en`, the pipeline's output, never
  `final_en`: a slip the reviewer fixed by hand is still the pipeline's slip.
