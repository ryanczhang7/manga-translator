## Context

The second of the two High Contrast stories (MT-026 is the first). MT-026 built
`resolve`; this story is everything that calls it — detection, stylesheet
re-composition, the canvas, focus, and a live mode change that must not cost the
user a sentence they were typing.

Floor: `docs/wiki/design/accessibility.md` **A-15.4 through A-15.10**, with the
reasoning in `docs/wiki/design/high-contrast.md` §2, §3, §6, §7 and §8.

Two things from the design document set the shape of the work, and both are
counter-intuitive enough to be worth stating before the criteria:

- **The stylesheet overriding the palette is the bug, not the mechanism.**
  `QApplication.setStyleSheet` wins over `QPalette`, so a fully QSS-themed app
  keeps painting its own colours while a contrast theme is active. The app does
  **not** drop or narrow the stylesheet under High Contrast; it **re-composes**
  it from resolved values. `high-contrast.md` §2, §2.1.
- **The canvas markers do not change, and that is the decision.** Every
  `overlay.*` token is `@static`. The markers are painted onto the user's
  artwork, which a contrast theme cannot reach, and the existing two-stroke
  halo-plus-core mechanism already guarantees legibility over both black and
  white art — a stronger guarantee than High Contrast gives chrome.
  `high-contrast.md` §7. **Exactly three** things change on the canvas and the
  markers are not among them: dimming stops, `color.canvas.frame` becomes
  `WindowText` at 2 px, and the viewport focus ring follows the theme — each
  with a runtime fall-back to its authored value if it fails 3:1 against the
  mat (§7.1, A-15.9).

**`depends_on` is MT-026 and MT-018**: MT-026 for `resolve`, MT-018 because the
cost readout's five states are among the surfaces this story has to get right
under a contrast theme, and they are the ones where colour was doing real work.

**Which required gate would fail if this story's artifact broke:** `unit`, which
runs `tests/ui/` under `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen`. That platform does not
report a contrast theme, which is precisely why detection must be injectable
(AC-1) — without that, nothing here is testable on CI and the story would be
verified only by someone's eyes on someone's desktop.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** — Given High Contrast reaches the app as **one boolean behind an
  interface**, when a test substitutes that interface, then the whole theme path
  runs under either setting with no display and no contrast theme present; and
  when nothing is injected, the default is **off**. *(A-15.4.)*
- **AC-2** — Given High Contrast is active, when the stylesheet is composed,
  then it is composed from `resolve(..., high_contrast=True, palette=...)` for
  every token and **applied** — the stylesheet is not dropped, not narrowed and
  not bypassed. *(A-15.3, `high-contrast.md` §2.1.)*
- **AC-3** — Given a real `QPalette`, when it is snapshotted for `resolve`, then
  the result is a plain `dict[str, str]` of `#RRGGBB` keyed by the five role
  names, and the adapter does nothing else. *(`high-contrast.md` §4.3.)*
- **AC-4** — Given High Contrast is active, when the canvas is painted, then
  every `overlay.*` colour and `color.canvas.surround` and
  `color.canvas.page-edge` are the **authored** values, unchanged.
  *(A-15.5, §6, §7.)*
- **AC-5** — Given High Contrast is active, when the canvas is painted, then
  **exactly three things differ** from the default theme and the markers are not
  among them: dimming stops (`overlay.dim.opacity` → 1.0), `color.canvas.frame`
  becomes `WindowText` at **2 px**, and the viewport focus ring follows High
  Contrast. *(`README.md` → "High Contrast — applying it to the screen"; A-15.5,
  A-15.6, §7.1.)*
- **AC-6** — Given High Contrast is active and a region is selected, when the
  canvas is painted, then **no marker is dimmed** and the selected marker still
  differs from every other by core stroke width, selection fill, and a filled
  ordinal badge. *(§7.1: three simultaneous non-hue signals, two of them shape
  and fill.)*
- **AC-7** — Given High Contrast is active, when each of the three canvas
  changes in AC-5 is resolved, then each **falls back to its authored value if
  it fails 3:1 against the `#5F5F5F` mat** — checked at runtime, because the mat
  is `@static` and the chrome is not, and that is the only place an HC colour
  meets a non-HC ground. *(A-15.9. Note this is a **fallback**, not an
  exception: `resolve` raises, this does not.)*
- **AC-8** — Given High Contrast is active, when each of the five `CostReadout`
  states is rendered, then all five are told apart by a unique
  **(figure text, glyph, meter fill)** triple and **never by hue**.
  *(`components.md` §10.6. `color.status.warning` collapses to `WindowText`, so
  a glyph that was decoration before is load-bearing now.)*
- **AC-9** — Given High Contrast is active, when each translation-row status is
  rendered (`proposed` / `accepted` / `edited` / `reverted` / `failed`), then all
  five are distinguishable in **one colour** — and `edited` versus `reverted`,
  which are **not** distinguishable as currently drawn, use the single return
  arrow `components.md` §10.4 specifies. *(A-15.7, §10.4.)*
- **AC-10** — Given High Contrast is active, when a widget takes focus, then the
  focus ring takes the **foreground of the ground it sits on** — `WindowText` on
  `Window`, `HighlightedText` on `Highlight`. *(A-15.8, A-04.)*
- **AC-11** — Given High Contrast is active, when the override sheet is applied,
  then hover is a **1 px `WindowText` outline and never a ground change**, a
  `ghost` button gains a boundary, and elevation drops the surface step and the
  `e2` drop shadow. *(`components.md` §10. Hover must stop being a ground change
  because hover and selection would otherwise both be `Highlight`.)*
- **AC-12** — Given the app is running with an open `LineEditor` holding
  **uncommitted text**, a selected region, a scrolled translation column and a
  panned, zoomed canvas, when the contrast theme is switched on or off, then the
  stylesheet is re-composed and re-applied, the canvas repaints, and **none of
  those four are lost**. *(A-15.10, §8 — the uncommitted text is the one that
  would actually hurt.)*
- **AC-13** — Given a live mode change, when it completes, then the live region
  announces exactly once ("High contrast on." / "High contrast off."), and **no
  notice, banner or toast is shown**. *(§8: under real support there is nothing
  to apologise for, and a notice would be an interruption.)*

## Contract

```
src/mangatl/ui/contrast.py
    class ContrastSource(Protocol):
        def is_high_contrast(self) -> bool: ...
        changed: Signal                       # fires on WM_SETTINGCHANGE
    class SystemContrastSource:               # SPI_GETHIGHCONTRAST
        ...
    class FixedContrastSource:                # the test double
        def __init__(self, value: bool = False) -> None

    def palette_snapshot(palette: QPalette) -> dict[str, str]

src/mangatl/ui/theme.py                       # extended by MT-028
    def compose_stylesheet(base_tmpl: str, hc_tmpl: str, *,
                           high_contrast: bool,
                           palette: Mapping[str, str]) -> str
    def apply_theme(app: QApplication, source: ContrastSource) -> None
    def mat_safe(value: str, authored: str) -> str   # AC-7: 3:1 or fall back
```

**AC-7 is a fallback, not a raise, and that asymmetry is deliberate.**
`resolve` raises on a missing role because a half-resolved *token set* is a
programming error a test can catch. The mat check is different: the input is a
**user's** contrast theme, which may legitimately be pathological, and refusing
to render because someone chose an unusual colour would be worse than quietly
using the authored value. `high-contrast.md` §6 / A-15.9.

**Settled by the design document, and to be read out:**

- **Detection is `SystemParametersInfo(SPI_GETHIGHCONTRAST)`, refreshed on
  `WM_SETTINGCHANGE`, behind an interface** (A-15.4). Default off. The offscreen
  platform reports nothing, so injectability is a requirement, not a
  convenience.
- **Re-compose, do not drop.** §2.1.
- **The canvas is exempt** except for dimming. §7, §7.1.
- **Announce once, show nothing.** §8.

**One thing is researched rather than observed, and this story is where it gets
settled.** A-15.11 and `high-contrast.md` §2.2 record that *Qt populates
`QPalette` from the contrast theme at all* has not been verified. **Read the real
palette on a real Windows session with a contrast theme active and record the
six values in `## Notes`.** If Qt does not populate it, the bounded fallback is
to read the Windows colours directly through `ctypes` — which changes the source
of the five-key dict and **nothing else**, because everything above
`palette_snapshot` takes a plain dict. Do not redesign anything else if this
falls the wrong way.

## Design notes

Cite `docs/wiki/design/accessibility.md` **A-15.4** through **A-15.10**, plus
**A-04**, **A-06**, **A-10** and **A-12**; the reasoning is in
`docs/wiki/design/high-contrast.md` §2, §3, §6, §7, §8.

The design point most likely to be "improved" by mistake: **the canvas markers
do not change under High Contrast.** The temptation is to run them through the
palette for consistency. Doing so would destroy the guarantee they already
provide, because they are drawn against a ground — the user's scan — that High
Contrast cannot touch, and the two-stroke mechanism was designed for exactly
that ground. §7 argues it at length; AC-4 and its mutation enforce it.

A limit recorded by the designer and **explicitly out of scope here**:
`color.canvas.page-edge` is a single 1 px near-black line and has no contrast on
a scan with a black gutter. That is a pre-existing gap in A-06's mechanism, not
something High Contrast introduced (§7.2), and it needs its own story.
