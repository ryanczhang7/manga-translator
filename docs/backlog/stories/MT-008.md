## Context

Open question **O6**, half of it. `docs/wiki/stack.md` §5/O6 and
`architecture.md` D11.

A region is an utterance: it gets its own line on the review screen, its own
translation, and its own bubble marker on the art. A phantom region is worse
than a missed one, because it costs the human attention on every page and
teaches them to distrust the markers.

**This story was retargeted in PLANNED, on measurement, before it left the
phase.** It was written as *"furigana columns are merged into their parent
region"*, on D11's premise that a detector emits a ruby column as its own
region. That premise does not hold for the detector MT-007 shipped, and the
phantom regions that *do* occur have a different cause. The measurement, the
decision and the user's approval are PO-1 in `## Notes`; the short version is:

- Across `011/012/014.jpg` the detector emits **30 regions from 25 boxes**. Every
  region falls inside exactly one box; there are no orphans.
- The four ruby columns on those pages (`ここ`, `まと`, `はしら` on 011, `しゅうき`
  on 014) are **all four absorbed** into their parent region by MT-007's
  dilation. Zero ruby columns survive as regions of their own.
- What *does* split is a bubble into its **vertical columns** — 7 of the 25
  boxes. `凄いよ人類` on 012 is two regions today: two review lines, two
  translations, two markers, one utterance.

So the rule this story builds merges **adjacent columns of one text block**.
Furigana folds in as a special case of the same rule rather than as a rule of
its own, and the epic's furigana promise is kept by AC-8, which pins the
absorption above as a standing regression guard rather than assuming it.

**Which gates would fail if this story's artifact broke.** Checked against
`bash scripts/gates.sh --list` rather than assumed, because the story's first
draft named `coverage-core` and was wrong:

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** — Given two regions inside one fence box whose edge-to-edge gap on
  the axis perpendicular to the run direction is at most `COLUMN_MAX_GAP_PX`,
  and whose extents along the run axis overlap by at least
  `COLUMN_MIN_OVERLAP` of the shorter of the two, when merging runs, then one
  region is produced whose polygon covers both and whose `merged_from` records
  the indices of both inputs.
- **AC-2** — Given two regions inside one fence box whose run-axis overlap is
  below `COLUMN_MIN_OVERLAP`, when merging runs, then **two** regions are
  produced and neither records a merge — *however small the gap*. This is the
  `011.jpg` box 4 case: two separate bubbles fenced by one box, gap 4 px,
  overlap 0.314.
- **AC-3** — Given two regions inside one fence box whose gap exceeds
  `COLUMN_MAX_GAP_PX`, when merging runs, then **two** regions are produced and
  neither records a merge — *however complete the overlap*. This is the
  `011.jpg` box 7 case: a separate `そう` bubble, gap 55 px, overlap 1.000.
- **AC-4** — Given two regions that satisfy both geometric conditions but lie in
  **different** fence boxes, when merging runs, then they are not merged.
- **AC-5** — Given a region that lies in no fence box, when merging runs, then
  it survives unmerged and unmodified. (Measured frequency on the corpus: 0 of
  30. The criterion fixes the behaviour anyway, because the safe direction is to
  keep an unexplained region rather than to attach it to a neighbour.)
- **AC-6** — Given a merged region, when its mask is inspected, then it is the
  union of the merged regions' masks and nothing else; and its `confidence` is
  the extent-weighted mean of the merged confidences.
- **AC-7** — Given `axis="horizontal"`, when merging runs, then every rule above
  applies on the transposed axis: the gap is measured vertically and the overlap
  horizontally.
- **AC-8** — *(integration)* Given the real detector and `011.jpg` and
  `014.jpg`, when detection and merging run, then no output region is confined
  to any of the four annotated ruby rectangles, and the output region count is
  strictly less than the pre-merge region count on both pages.
- **AC-9** — Given a merged region written to a project and read back, when the
  two are compared, then `merged_from` survives the round trip.

### C-1 — the new module

```python
# src/mangatl/detect/columns.py
COLUMN_MAX_GAP_PX: int        # settled in GREEN within the bounds C-4 measures
COLUMN_MIN_OVERLAP: float     # ditto

def merge_columns(
    regions: Sequence[RawRegion],
    fences: Sequence[tuple[int, int, int, int]],
    *,
    axis: Literal["vertical", "horizontal"] = "vertical",
) -> list[RawRegion]: ...
```

`fences` are half-open integer pixel rectangles `(x0, y0, x1, y1)` — exactly what
`postprocess._integer_boxes` already produces. **GREEN makes that function
public as `integer_boxes` and adds it to `postprocess.__all__`**; a plain
rectangle keeps `numpy` out of this signature and lets every core test build
fences by hand.

Merging is **transitive within a fence box**: three columns of one bubble
produce one region, not two. `merged_from` on the output carries every absorbed
input index, sorted ascending, and is `()` on a region that absorbed nothing.
Output order follows `regions_from_detection`'s convention — sorted by bounding
box `(y0, x0)`.

**Amended in RED — the six things a test at a boundary needs stated to the
pixel.** Each is a gap in the block above rather than a change to it; AC-1 to
AC-7 are untouched. `tests/core/test_detect_columns.py` pins all six, and its
module docstring repeats the first two where an implementer will read them.

1. **Extent.** A region's extent is the **half-open bounding box of its set mask
   pixels**, `x0 <= x < x1` and `y0 <= y < y1` — the box-membership convention
   MT-007 amendment A-6 already uses. For everything `regions_from_detection`
   emits this is also the polygon's bounding box, because `_polygon` is the
   contour of those same pixels.
2. **The two quantities, as formulas.** With `axis="vertical"`, for extents `a`
   and `b`: `gap = max(0, max(ax0, bx0) - min(ax1, bx1))` — the count of empty
   pixel columns between them, so touching or overlapping extents both score 0 —
   and `overlap = max(0, min(ay1, by1) - max(ay0, by0))` shared rows, taken as a
   fraction of `min(ay1 - ay0, by1 - by0)`, the **shorter** height.
   `axis="horizontal"` swaps the two axes. Without this, "gap 9" is ambiguous by
   one pixel and a correct implementation and an off-by-one one both satisfy C-4,
   whose separations are 6.1× and 3.2×.
3. **Which fence a region is in.** The fence whose rectangle has the greatest
   area of intersection with the region's extent; ties to the **lowest** fence
   index, which is `decode_boxes`'s most confident box and the same tie-break
   `postprocess._kind` already makes; and **no fence** when that area is zero for
   every rectangle, which is AC-5's case. Measured on the corpus, all three
   candidate rules — containment, greatest mask overlap, greatest extent overlap
   — agree on all 30 regions, so this is a choice between rules nothing
   distinguishes rather than a finding.
4. **Transitivity, precisely.** The pairwise relation is evaluated on the
   **input** extents, and the output is one region per connected component of
   that graph. Re-evaluating against a growing merged extent would make the
   result depend on the order pairs were visited.
5. **The merged region's other fields.** Its polygon is a closed ring whose
   bounding box is the union of the merged extents — the shape inside that box
   is GREEN's to choose, and the mask is what carries the detail. Its `kind` is
   the `kind` of the **lowest-indexed** input; in the pipeline they always agree,
   because `kind` comes from the fencing box, but the function must still answer
   deterministically.
6. **"Extent-weighted" in AC-6 means weighted by set mask pixels**, not by
   bounding-box area: `confidence` is defined as the mean probability inside the
   mask (MT-007 C-7), so weighting the two means by their own pixel counts is the
   one combination that equals the mean over the union. An L-shaped fixture in
   `test_a_merged_confidence_is_the_mean_weighted_by_the_masks_own_pixel_counts`
   separates the two readings: 0.63333 by mask pixels, 0.65294 by bounding-box
   area, 0.55 unweighted.

`postprocess.py:282`, not 280. One caller, inside its own module, as stated.

### C-2 — the domain change

```python
# src/mangatl/domain/region.py
RawRegion.merged_from: tuple[int, ...] = ()   # indices into the input sequence
```

Indices, not ids: regions have no database identity until they are written, and
`merged_from` is computed before the write. The default is `()` so that every
existing construction site keeps compiling — **which is precisely why C-5 exists**.
`__post_init__` must reject a non-`int`, a negative, or a duplicated index, for
the reason the existing `int` check gives: `numpy.int32` is not an `int` and
`json.dumps` refuses it, and this field is JSON on its way to the store.

### C-3 — which axis is which

Run direction is a **parameter, not an inference.** The first draft of this
story derived it from each region's aspect ratio and called a transposition "the
default mistake here"; the measurement shows aspect ratio cannot carry it —
`011.jpg` box 4 holds a 134×204 region (taller) beside a 181×156 region (wider),
in one box, in a page that is vertical throughout. The corpus is 100% vertical
text, so there is **no evidence for any inference rule**, and inventing one
would be a claim this story cannot support.

- `axis="vertical"` (the default, and what the pipeline passes): columns run
  top-to-bottom, so the **gap is horizontal** (on `x`) and the **overlap is
  vertical** (on `y`).
- `axis="horizontal"`: transposed.

AC-7 is therefore mechanical and synthetic, and it is a sharp test of the axis
logic rather than of the merge logic.

### C-6 — the store

`region.merged_from` already exists in `schema.py` as a nullable `TEXT` column,
added by MT-005 against this story. MT-008 makes it real, and the read path must
read what it writes (`architecture.md` D12).

- `_UPSERT_REGION` gains `merged_from`, as JSON, **in both the `INSERT` column

**Amended in RED — an empty `merged_from` is written as SQL `NULL`, not as
`"[]"`.** This is forced rather than chosen. `tests/core/test_region_store.py`
line 130 is a frozen MT-007 assertion that three regions written through
`write_regions` read back as `[None, None, None]` in the `merged_from` column,
observed through a plain `sqlite3` connection. A writer that stored `"[]"` for a
region that absorbed nothing would fail that assertion, in a file GREEN may not
edit — the story would come back to RED for a test that is not wrong.
`test_the_merge_on_disk_is_json_and_an_absent_merge_is_null` pins both halves:
JSON for a real merge, `NULL` for none.

**This supersedes the comment at `store/project.py:124**` ("`merged_from` is left
out of the statement entirely, so a re-detection cannot clear a merge MT-008
recorded"). That comment anticipated merging as a separate later pass over
stored rows. It is not: the pipeline order is detect → merge → write, so
`write_regions` only ever receives already-merged regions and `reading_index` is
an index over them. A re-detection therefore produces a fresh merge set, and the
previous one is stale rather than precious — omitting the field from the `DO
UPDATE` would preserve a merge record against regions that no longer exist.
GREEN replaces that comment with this reasoning. PO-2 in `## Notes`.

**Why AC-9 is not optional.** With a `()` default on both sides, every existing
round-trip equality assertion in `tests/core/test_region_store.py` passes whether
or not the field is persisted. AC-9 is the only thing standing between this story
and a silent data-loss path that MT-009 would inherit.

**Finding 1 — AC-4 of the first draft failed its own control.** It required "a

utterance. `凄いよ人類` on 012 is two regions: two review lines, two
translations, two markers, one utterance. The first draft's **AC-2 required that
to stay split** — comparable extent ⇒ two regions, no merge. The story as
written would have shipped a rule that fires on nothing observable while being
contractually forbidden from fixing what is broken.

**Finding 3 — the fence box is a real signal, and is not sufficient.** Every one
of the 30 regions falls inside exactly one box, so regions in different boxes are
different text blocks by the box head's own judgement (AC-4). But `011` box 4
fences **two separate bubbles**, so sharing a box does not imply one utterance
(AC-2). Hence the conjunction in C-5.

that made retargeting the honest option rather than a preference.

before this story, by MT-007's dilation rather than by anything MT-008 does.
Silently dropping it would leave the epic's clause unattached to any test, so
AC-8 pins it as a standing regression guard on the real detector. That is the
numbered decision the gap-closing rule asks for.

**Known limitation, recorded rather than resolved.** Three pages, one artist,
one scan resolution, four ruby instances, seven same-box pairs. The thresholds
in C-4 are separated by 6.1× and 3.2×, which is healthy, but the corpus is small
and entirely vertical text. A page whose box fences two bubbles *side by side at
the same height* would be merged wrongly by this rule, and no such page is in the
corpus — a ratio test would not have caught it either, since two adjacent bubbles
have comparable extents. If one appears, that is a new story with new evidence.
