## Context

Filed 2026-10-28 from the user's report. After baking their 4-page `mato
sample` chapter, the Render dialog said *"13 lines do not fit their bubble
and will be cut off (pages 1, 2, 3, 4)"* of 20, the output English is tiny
and clipped at both sides ("I s pposes it's hot spring olomac", "This
ody.."), and the user: *"not only does the text cut off in the bubbles, but
gives way too much empty space to the bubbles. there is a lot more space to
be used in the bubbles. i would accept a certain amount of variation in font
size."*

**The defect, measured on the user's four pages (2026-10-28, read-only copy
of `mato sample.mtproj/project.db`; script in the orchestrator's scratchpad,
numbers in `## Notes`).** `bake._set_blocks` calls
`typeset(text, region.polygon, faces)`, and `region.polygon` is the
**detector's text region** - the outer contour of the dilated Japanese
column (`detect.postprocess._polygon`, or the merged columns' bounding box,
`detect.columns._merge`). It is never the bubble. On the 17 bubbles of the
sample the bubble's white interior is **2.6x to 8.8x** the area of the
polygon's bounding box. `fit.fitting_area` then insets that column by a
further 12% of its short side, so the fitting area of a 71 x 205 px column is
54 x 164 px: 13 of 18 lines land on the 14 px floor, 13 overflow, and the
median fill (text block area / bubble interior) is **0.084**. Overflowed
blocks are laid out at the polygon's width and clipped by the bake area
(erase mask united with polygon fill, MT-021 C-3), which is the clipping the
user saw. The hypothesis in the report held exactly.

**Nothing stored knows where the bubble is.** The detector's box head is a
text-block box, not a bubble outline (`postprocess.py` header, MT-007 C-3),
and it is not persisted. The bubble has to be found from the **cleaned
page**, where the Japanese is already erased and the interior is flat white
inside a dark outline. Two methods were measured on the sample (`## Notes`):
a flood of light pixels from the polygon finds 12 of 17 interiors exactly and
leaks on the 5 bubbles drawn open against a panel edge; a ray cast capped at
the polygon's own size holds those 5. Flood-first, cap-bounded star as the
fallback: **0 of 17 overflow, 0 of 489 glyph boxes on outline or art, median
fill 0.31**, sizes 19-41 px. That rule is the `## Contract`. It is a
derivation from four pages, so RED may amend its numbers in place with a
reason; what it may not do is re-derive the fitting tokens, which are design
decisions (`typeset-font.md` §7, `tokens.toml` `[typeset]`).

Bounded by `architecture.md` §3 (`typeset` imports nothing of `detect` or
`clean`; `coverage-core` holds `typeset/**` at 100%) and EPIC-06's non-goal:
no per-bubble control of anything - the interior is found, never drawn or
dragged. Text outside bubbles (`kind == "box"`: captions, the chapter title)
keeps today's behaviour, by the user's instruction.

**Required gates that would fail if the artifact broke:** `unit`,
`coverage-core` (`typeset/interior.py` at 100%), and **`integration`**,
binding here through the frontmatter: the story's central claim - the user's
four pages come out unclipped and filled - can only be checked on those
pages, which are non-redistributable scans under gitignored `spikes/**`
(`story-authoring`, "When the evidence lives in an optional gate"). The pages
need no GPU, so they get their own marker, `pages`, and the gate's `-m`
filter widens to carry it (`## Contract`, block 5).

## Acceptance criteria

Numbers in AC-1 to AC-4 are the Contract's; RED reads them out. AC-5 is the
real-page oracle with its decision rule written here, first.

- **AC-1** - *A closed bubble.* Given a cleaned page whose pixels are a
  non-light texture (grey < 200 everywhere) except for one ellipse of
  **300 x 240 px** drawn as a **4 px dark outline** (grey <= 120) around a
  white (255) interior, and a region polygon that is a **60 x 200 px**
  rectangle centred in it, when `bubble_interior(page, polygon, "bubble")` is
  called, then the returned ring's fill covers **>= 95%** of the white
  interior's pixels and **no** outline pixel, and `typeset(text, ring, faces)`
  for a text that `typeset(text, polygon, faces)` sets at `MIN_SIZE_PX`
  overflowed sets it **not overflowed at >= 2 x `MIN_SIZE_PX`**.
- **AC-2** - *An open bubble is bounded, not leaked.* Given the page of AC-1
  with the ellipse's outline **broken by a 60 px gap** that opens onto a
  white (255) region reaching the page edge, when `bubble_interior` is
  called, then the returned ring contains no outline pixel, every vertex
  lies inside the **window** (the polygon's bounding box grown by
  `CAP_RATIO x max(w, h)` on each side, clipped to the page), and no vertex
  lies on the page edge. *Control:* the plain light-pixel flood of the same
  page (the test's own oracle, Contract block 6) reaches the page edge - that
  is the leak this criterion refuses.
- **AC-3** - *Fallbacks are the identity.* (a) Given any page and polygon,
  when `kind == "box"`, then the polygon comes back **unchanged** (`==`).
  (b) Given a page whose only light pixels inside the polygon form a
  component **smaller than the polygon's fill** (the synthetic bake world:
  `tests/core/_bake_world.texture` with the erase mask flat white), when
  `kind == "bubble"`, then the polygon comes back unchanged. (c) Given a page
  with **no** light pixel inside the polygon, the polygon comes back
  unchanged. Consequence, asserted as a behaviour: every existing test in
  `tests/core/test_bake.py`, `test_bake_font.py` and `test_cli_bake.py`
  passes unmodified, because on that world the interior **is** the polygon
  and the baked bytes are the bytes they already pin.
- **AC-4** - *The bake uses it, and the clip follows it.* Given a project

  positions **outside the polygon's fill and inside the ring's fill**, (b)

  **empty** - the dialog and the output read the same rule. *Control:* with
  the line's text long enough to overflow even the interior, `overflowing`
  names it and no ink lies outside that same union.
- **AC-5** - *The user's pages.* Given the four sample pages and their

### 1. `src/mangatl/typeset/interior.py` - new module

```python
from typing import Literal
import numpy as np
from numpy.typing import NDArray
from mangatl.typeset.fit import Polygon  # Sequence[tuple[float, float]]

LIGHT_GREY: int = 200   # a cleaned-page pixel at or above this is bubble white
DARK_GREY: int = 120    # a pixel at or below this is outline ink
CAP_RATIO: float = 1.0  # the window grows the polygon's bbox by this x max(w, h) per side
RAY_COUNT: int = 72     # rays of the fallback star, at equal angles from the bbox centre

Ring = tuple[tuple[int, int], ...]  # closed: last vertex repeats the first, >= 4 vertices

def bubble_interior(
    cleaned: NDArray[np.uint8],          # (H, W) grey of the CLEANED page, y down
    polygon: Polygon,                    # the region's polygon, page pixels
    kind: Literal["bubble", "box"],
) -> Ring: ...
```

Semantics, in order; the first rule that applies returns:

1. `kind == "box"` -> `tuple(polygon)` unchanged. Text on art stays where
   the detector put it (user, 2026-10-28).
2. *Window* `W` = the polygon's bounding box grown by `CAP_RATIO x max(w, h)`
   on every side, clipped to the page. Nothing returned ever leaves `W`.
3. *Flood.* `light = cleaned >= LIGHT_GREY`; 4-connected components of
   `light`; *seed* = `light` inside the polygon's fill (`cv2.fillPoly`).
   No seed -> polygon unchanged (rule 3c of AC-3). `C` = the component
   holding the most seed pixels.
4. `area(C) < area(polygon fill)` -> polygon unchanged. A bubble is bigger
   than its text; a component smaller than the column is noise, a dark
   background, or the synthetic world's flat-white erase mask - and in each
   case today's behaviour is the right one.
5. `C`'s bounding box strictly inside `W` and touching no page edge ->
   interior = `C | polygon fill`.
6. Otherwise the flood **leaked** (a bubble drawn open against a panel edge
   or the gutter), and the interior is the **star**: `dark = cleaned <=
   DARK_GREY`, closed once by a 3 x 3 kernel (`cv2.MORPH_CLOSE`) so a
   1-px gap in an outline does not pass a ray; from the centre of the
   polygon's bounding box, `RAY_COUNT` rays at equal angles, each advancing
   1 px until the pixel is dark, is off the page, or is `CAP_RATIO x
   max(w, h)` from the centre; the last light on-page pixel of each ray is a
   vertex. interior = (star fill `& ~dark`) `| polygon fill`, with `dark` the
   closed mask just described. *(Amended in RED, 2026-10-29: the rule as
   first written was "interior = star fill `| polygon fill`". On the AC-2
   page it puts **67 outline pixels** in the ring's fill - the straight chord
   between a ray that stopped on the outline beside the gap and its
   neighbour that ran through the gap cuts across the outline's end - so
   AC-2's "no outline pixel" was unsatisfiable by the Contract's own rule.
   Removing the dark mask from the star before the union: **0** outline
   pixels, cover of the open bubble's white 0.9957, and on the user's pages
   only `009/2` moves (25 -> 26 px); 0 overflow, 0/489 boxes on dark,
   medians 0.330 / 0.345 / 0.342 / 0.246 unchanged - measured with a scratch
   implementation of this block, `## Handoff`.)*
7. Return the **outer contour** of the interior (`cv2.findContours`,
   `RETR_EXTERNAL`, `CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE`, the largest by area) as a closed
   ring of Python `int`s - the same conventions `RawRegion.polygon` carries
   (`domain/region.py`). The union in 5 and 6 is what makes the interior
   always cover the polygon fill, so the bake area below is monotone.

Measured on the sample under exactly this rule: flood on 12 bubbles, star
on 5; sizes 19-41 px; fill per page 0.33 / 0.345 / 0.342 / 0.246; 0 glyph
boxes on dark pixels (`## Notes`, table 3). `cv2` is already a dependency of
`detect`, `clean` and `pipeline`; `typeset` importing it breaks no contract
in `pyproject.toml` (`[tool.importlinter]` forbids `cv2` nowhere). The
module imports `numpy` and `cv2` only - nothing of `mangatl.detect` or
`mangatl.clean` (layers contract).
