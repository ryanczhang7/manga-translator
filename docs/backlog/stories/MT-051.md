## Context

**Q2 and Q3 answered by the user on 2026-09-29 (see `## Notes`, "User decisions"). AC-3 and AC-7 state those answers, and they freeze when the story leaves PLANNED.**

The ordinal badge is the **primary carrier of the bubble ↔ line link**
(`docs/wiki/design/components.md` §4.2). A badge hidden under another badge breaks the
link for that bubble on exactly the dense, overlapping pages where it matters most.
§4.2 is normative:

> Badges never overlap each other. If a badge's circle would intersect an already-placed
> badge, it slides along its own region's outline (clockwise, in `overlay.badge.size / 2`
> steps) until it does not; if a full circuit finds no free position, it is placed at the
> region's centroid. Placement is deterministic in ordinal order, so the same page always
> produces the same layout.

MT-016 draws every badge at its nominal position: a circle of `overlay.badge.size`
(18 px) just off the region's bounding-rect top-right corner, offset `overlay.badge.gap`
(4 px). It is drawn at a **constant on-screen size** (MT-016 GREEN note: the painter is
scaled by `1 / m11()`, matching the cosmetic pens). Collision avoidance was explicitly
not in MT-016 (Contract, `paint_marker` step 4).

Two readings are settled here, not guessed, because each follows from a normative
clause:

- **Where a badge would sit if nothing collided is unchanged.** A badge that collides
  with nothing stays exactly where MT-016 draws it. That is the "nominal" position, and
  AC-1 pins it so that this story cannot move every badge on a page to fix a few.
- **"Intersect" means that the circles' interiors overlap:** the distance between
  centres is less than `overlay.badge.size`. Two badges that touch do not intersect. The
  halo stroke is not counted as part of the circle.

The oracle for the pure placement function is a hand computation on simple polygons,
which makes this the most testable part of the MT-016 follow-up. That is why it is a
story of its own.

**Which required gate would fail if this story's artifact broke:** `unit`
(`tests/ui/`). The placement function is Qt-free and runs under `tests/ui/` with the
rest of the link.

## Acceptance criteria

All coordinates below are in **zoomed-scene units**: page pixels × the current zoom,
which is what one screen pixel measures (see Q2). "Size" is `overlay.badge.size` and
"gap" is `overlay.badge.gap`, read from `tokens_gen`.

- **AC-1** — Given a page whose regions' nominal badges do not intersect one another,
  when badges are placed, then every badge is at its nominal position: the circle whose
  bottom-left corner is the region's bounding-rect top-right corner offset by
  (+gap, −gap), which is MT-016's position. Nothing moves when nothing collides.
- **AC-2** — Given any page, when badges are placed, then every badge that was *not*
  placed by the centroid fallback is disjoint from every badge placed before it (centre
  distance ≥ size).
  *Control:* the MT-016 placement (every badge nominal) must fail this on a fixture of
  two regions whose top-right corners coincide.
- **AC-3** — Given a region whose nominal badge intersects an already-placed badge,
  when it is placed, then its badge is at the **first** candidate along its own outline,
  walking clockwise from the start point in steps of size/2, whose circle intersects no
  already-placed badge. The walk starts at the outline point nearest the region's
  bounding-rect top-right corner, advances 9 px of outline length per step (size/2,
  size 18, in screen px at the current zoom), and each candidate centre sits 13 px
  (gap 4 + size/2) outward from the outline along its normal (Q3, answered
  2026-09-29). The test
  asserts exact coordinates computed by hand on a rectangle and on an irregular polygon,
  not coordinates obtained by calling the function.
  *Control:* a walk in steps of size (not size/2) and a counter-clockwise walk must each
  fail it.
- **AC-4** — Given the same region stored with clockwise vertex order and with
  counter-clockwise vertex order, when badges are placed, then the placement is
  identical. "Clockwise" means clockwise **as seen on screen** (y down), whatever the
  stored winding.
- **AC-5** — Given a region for which every candidate on the full circuit intersects an
  already-placed badge, when it is placed, then its badge is centred on the region's
  **area centroid** (the polygon centroid computed by the shoelace formula).
  *Control:* the fixture's polygon is irregular enough that the vertex mean differs from
  the area centroid by at least size/2, so a vertex-mean implementation fails.
  **Degenerate rings (Amendment 1):** a region whose ring has zero area (every vertex
  collinear, or a single repeated point) has no area centroid and no clockwise direction.
  If its nominal badge intersects an already-placed badge, the walk is skipped and its
  badge is centred on its **bounding-rect centre** (placement `CENTROID`). Placing any
  page raises no exception.
- **AC-6** — Given a page, when badges are placed, then the result depends only on the
  regions (their ordinals and polygons) and the zoom. Placing the same regions supplied
  in a shuffled sequence order gives the same result, and a badge's position never
  depends on any region with a higher ordinal (removing the highest-ordinal region
  leaves every other badge where it was).
- **AC-7** — Given the canvas showing a page whose nominal badges collide, when the
  markers are painted at a zoom, then the badge circles actually drawn (captured paint
  calls, mapped to device coordinates) are exactly the placed positions and pairwise
  satisfy AC-2. After `set_zoom` to a different factor, the painted positions are those
  placed at the new zoom (Q2).
  *Control:* MT-016's shipped `paint_marker` (nominal badges) fails the first half. This
  is the criterion that stops a correct pure function from shipping unused.

**The placement rule, every number read out (all in zoomed-scene units):**

1. **Polygon normalisation.** A trailing vertex equal to the first (the closed rings
   `OrderedRegion.polygon` stores) is dropped; zero-length edges are skipped. The
   domain is every ring `RawRegion` accepts: a closed ring of ≥ 4 stored vertices
   (≥ 1 distinct point) with non-negative int page coordinates, zero area included
   (Amendment 1; the earlier "≥ 3 distinct vertices, non-zero area" narrowing had no
   design clause behind it). **Zero area** is the shoelace sum being exactly 0,
   computed on the input (page-px) ring before the zoom is applied.
   **Screen-clockwise** (y down) is the traversal whose shoelace sum
   Σ(xᵢ·yᵢ₊₁ − xᵢ₊₁·yᵢ) is **positive**; a polygon with a negative sum is traversed
   in reverse. Stored winding therefore never matters (AC-4).
2. **Nominal (candidate 0).** With the bounding rect's right edge `R` and top edge
   `T`: centre = `(R + gap + size/2, T − gap − size/2)` = `(R + 13, T − 13)` at size
   18, gap 4. This is MT-016's circle (`QRectF(gap, −gap − size, size, size)` about
   the top-right corner).
3. **Intersects** ⇔ centre distance **< size** (strictly). Touching (= size) is free.
4. **Walk.** Start point `S` = the outline point nearest the bounding-rect top-right
   corner `(R, T)` (Euclidean). Ties — RED's fixtures must avoid them — go to the
   first found scanning the screen-clockwise edges from the normalised first vertex.
   Candidate *k*, for *k* = 1, 2, … while *k*·(size/2) < perimeter, is the point at
   arc length *k*·(size/2) = *k*·9 clockwise from `S`, displaced by **gap + size/2 =
   13** along the outward unit normal of the edge that point lies on. At a vertex
   exactly, the edge is the one being **entered** (leaving the vertex clockwise).
   For a screen-clockwise edge with direction `(dx, dy)`, the outward normal is
   `(dy, −dx)/|d|` (the top edge of a rectangle, walking right, points up).
5. **Choice.** Nominal if it intersects no placed badge → `NOMINAL`. Else the first
   walk candidate that intersects none → `SLID`. Else the area centroid → `CENTROID`.
   A centroid badge is placed anyway and **does** count as "already placed" for later
   ordinals.
   **Zero-area ring (Amendment 1):** nominal if free → `NOMINAL`; else no walk, the
   bounding-rect centre `((L + R)/2, (T + B)/2)` in zoomed-scene units → `CENTROID`.
   `area_centroid` itself is only called on non-zero-area rings.
6. Expected coordinates are compared with `pytest.approx(abs=1e-6)`.

## Amendments

- **Amendment 1 — AC-5 (2026-09-29, approved by the user in chat, option "Fix in this
  story").** *Was:* AC-5 covered only a region with an area centroid, and the Contract
  narrowed the domain to "≥ 3 distinct vertices and non-zero area". *Now:* AC-5 adds
  "Degenerate rings": a zero-area ring whose nominal badge collides skips the walk and
  is badged at its bounding-rect centre (`CENTROID`), and placing any page raises no
  exception. The Contract domain is every ring `RawRegion` accepts. *Why:* the Lead PO
  reproduced a `ZeroDivisionError` on a single-point ring in GREEN (see `## Notes`,
  "Degenerate rings"). `RawRegion` and the store represent such rings, so the
  narrowing had no design clause behind it (rules.md), and MT-016 never raised on them.
  A zero-area ring has no clockwise direction, so the walk is undefined for it; the
  bounding-rect centre is defined for every ring.

## Notes

### Open questions (answered 2026-09-29; see "User decisions" below)

- **Q2 — In which space are badges placed, and is the layout the same at every zoom?**
  Badges are drawn at a constant *screen* size (18 px at every zoom), while regions
  scale with zoom. At 10% zoom, badges that were far apart at 100% can overlap. §4.2
  says both "badges never overlap" and "the same page always produces the same layout",
  and at a fixed badge screen size both cannot hold at every zoom. The readings lead to
  different code:
  - **(a) Recommended:** place badges in zoomed-scene units (page px × zoom) and
    re-place them whenever the zoom changes. Badges never overlap at any zoom, and the
    layout is deterministic for a given (page, zoom). Panning never moves a badge.
  - (b) Place once, at zoom 1.0, in page pixels, and keep that layout at every zoom. The
    layout is stable, but badges overlap when zoomed out.
  - (c) Make badges scale with the page (scene-sized). This contradicts MT-016's
    constant-size badge and the "px" reading of `overlay.badge.size` in `tokens.md`.
- **Q3 — What exactly is the sliding path?** §4.2 says "slides along its own region's
  outline". It does not say where the slide starts, or how the badge circle sits relative
  to the outline while it slides. The nominal position is *off* the bounding-rect corner,
  not on the outline. Recommended reading, for the designer to confirm or replace:
  - candidate 0 is the nominal position (AC-1);
  - the walk starts at the outline point nearest the bounding-rect top-right corner
    (for a rectangle, that corner);
  - candidate *k* (for *k* ≥ 1, while *k*·size/2 is less than the perimeter) has its
    centre at the outline point *k*·(size/2) of arc length clockwise from the start,
    displaced along the outward normal of the edge that point lies on by
    gap + size/2. At a vertex, the normal is that of the edge being entered. This keeps
    the circle `gap` clear of the outline, as the nominal one is;
  - "a full circuit" means every such *k*.

  The alternative, with the circle centred *on* the outline point, is simpler but covers
  the stroke the badge is supposed to label. It produces different coordinates, so AC-3
  cannot be frozen until this is answered.

### Readings the Lead PO took, and why they are not open

- Intersection is interior overlap (centre distance < size), and touching is allowed.
  This is ordinary geometry, and the halo is a stroke, not part of the "circle".
- The centroid is the area centroid. "A region's centroid", without a qualifier, means
  the centroid of the region. The mean of its vertices is a different point.
- Clockwise is judged on screen. The design is about what the user sees, and the stored
  winding of a detected polygon is an accident of the detector.

### Degenerate rings — escalated to the user (2026-09-29, during GREEN)

The feature-developer reported that a degenerate region could crash placement. The
Lead PO reproduced it independently, on different inputs, calling the shipped
`place_badges` directly (not the subagent's probe):

```
collinear, nominal collides     -> {0: NOMINAL (53, -13), 1: SLID (31, 13)}
single point, nominal collides  -> RAISES ZeroDivisionError float division by zero
collinear, alone                -> {0: NOMINAL (53, -8)}
```

A collinear (zero-area) ring also divides by zero in `area_centroid` if every candidate
is blocked. **Reachability:** `RawRegion.__post_init__` accepts any closed ring of
≥ 4 vertices with non-negative int coordinates, including zero-area and single-point
rings, and the store round-trips them. The default detector path dilates every
component before `_polygon` (postprocess.py), so real detection is unlikely to emit
one. But the Contract's "non-zero area, ≥ 3 distinct vertices" domain is narrower than
the type the design represents. rules.md allows a narrowing only when the design
cannot represent what was excluded, and here it can. MT-016's painter never raised on
such rings. So this is a crash regression on representable input, and the decision
goes to the user.
