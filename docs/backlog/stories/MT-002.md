## Context

`docs/wiki/stack.md` §3 names this the largest single risk in the plan, and
`architecture.md` D3 records the decision it rests on. The claim is:

> Usable ONNX exports of a comic text detector, `manga-ocr` and LaMa exist, and
> run on an RTX 5070 (Blackwell, sm_120) under `onnxruntime-gpu`.

That claim is **researched, not verified**. Three later epics depend on it, and
it decides a dependency in `pyproject.toml` and roughly 2 GB of installer size.
A spike is the right shape: the output is a decision document, not code.

The named fallback if it fails is `torch` + `torchvision` on the `cu128` index
(Blackwell needs CUDA 12.8+), at the cost of a much larger installer.

A spike's phase path is PLANNED → REVIEW → DONE. Any code it writes is throwaway
and lives under `spikes/MT-002/`, which `.gitignore` covers — so if it is
committed at all, it goes in `## Scaffold inventory` and is explicitly labelled
unreviewed. **Nothing later may reuse it without re-deriving it.**

## Acceptance criteria

A spike's criteria are about what is *known* at the end, not about behaviour.

- **AC-1** — `docs/wiki/audits/MT-002-model-runtime.md` exists and states, for
  each of the three models, whether a usable ONNX export was obtained, where
  from, its file hash, and its licence.
- **AC-2** — For each model obtained, the document records a measured forward
  pass on a real manga page fixture on this machine: execution provider
  selected, wall time, peak VRAM, and whether the output was plausible (a
  detector producing regions, the OCR producing Japanese, LaMa producing
  non-garbage pixels).
- **AC-3** — The document records what `onnxruntime.get_available_providers()`
  actually returns on this machine, and whether the CUDA execution provider
  loaded or fell through to DirectML or CPU.
- **AC-4** — The document ends with a `## Decided` section naming the runtime
  for EPIC-03 and EPIC-06 — ONNX Runtime or the PyTorch fallback — the
  dependency lines to add to `pyproject.toml`, and the estimated installed size
  of the bundled weights.
- **AC-5** — Anything the document reports as a *measurement* is labelled with
  what it was measured on, and anything it reports as a *claim from the web* is
  labelled as such. A number with no provenance is a guess with a citation.

## Contract

**Output paths:**

- `docs/wiki/audits/MT-002-model-runtime.md` — the decision document.
- `docs/wiki/stack.md` §3 — amended in place with the settled dependency.
- `spikes/MT-002/` — throwaway probe scripts, if any survive.

**The `## Decided` / `## Evidence` split is load-bearing.** Later stories will
be told "read the audit and follow its recommendation; do not re-litigate it" —
and that instruction is correct about `## Decided` and wrong about
`## Evidence`. Put every *number* a later story might depend on (a latency, a
VRAM figure, a threshold, a claim that two exports agree) under `## Evidence`,
where it is to be re-verified rather than assumed. `story-authoring` carries the
case where an audit's recommendation was right and its supporting number failed
for 58% of inputs.

**What a later story may take from this spike, stated now so it does not have to
be argued later:** the *choice of runtime* and the *model file identities*
(URL + hash). Nothing else. Any pre-processing code written here is unreviewed
and must be re-derived test-first in MT-007, MT-010 and MT-019.

**Oracle partition: oracle-free.** There is no settled answer to read out; the
spike's job is to find out. So the demand is on the *negative* side: a "plausible
output" claim must say what would have counted as implausible, or it is an
impression.

### PO-1 — MT-002 stays document-only; the first integration test moves to MT-007

**Decided 2026-09-12 by lead-po, put to the user before any work ran, and
approved by them.**

Three places in the tree disagreed about MT-002's scope:

- `tests/integration/README.md`: *"MT-002 writes the first test here and the
  waiver comes out then."*
- `.claude/harness/project.conf`, twice — the `waiver | integration` comment
  (*"tests/integration/ is empty until MT-002 … MT-002 writes the first
  real-model smoke test and REMOVES THIS LINE"*) and the `discovery | providers`
  comment (*"EXPECTED TO REPORT MISSING until MT-002"*).
- This story: `type: spike`, *"the output is a decision document, not code"*,
  AC-4 says the document records **the dependency lines to add** to
  `pyproject.toml` rather than adding them, and *Out of scope* opens with *"Any
  production code."*

The story wins, on three independent grounds:

1. **The phase lock already agrees with it.** `paths.conf` classifies
   `fixtures/**` and `**/tests/**` as `test`; `phases.conf` gives a `spike` the
   path PLANNED → REVIEW → DONE, and none of those three phases permits a `test`
   write. Making MT-002 write `tests/integration/` needs either `--force` or a
   phase the story's own type does not have. A scope that can only be executed
   by routing around the lock is the lock telling you the scope is wrong
   (law 5).
2. **`stack.md`'s own sizing rule forbids the dependency here.** *"MT-001's
   sizing rule forbids adding a dependency nothing imports"* — and nothing
   imports `onnxruntime` until MT-007. Pinning ~2 GB of inference runtime in
   `pyproject.toml` at MT-002 would put it in `uv.lock`, in every `uv sync`, and
   in the `build` gate's PyInstaller run, for three stories before the first
   line of code reads it.
3. **MT-007 already owns it, precisely.** It is `type: feature`,
   `depends_on: [MT-002]`, `required_gates: [integration]`, and its **AC-7** is
   verbatim *"Given the real detector model and a real page fixture, when the
   session loads and runs…"*. That **is** the first integration test. MT-001's
   comments were a correct prediction about *what must happen* attached to the
   wrong story id.

**What this story therefore does not do:** touch `pyproject.toml` or `uv.lock`,
write anything under `tests/`, or remove the `integration` waiver. The
`providers` discovery line stays MISSING after MT-002 and that is now the
documented expectation.

**What changed as a result of this decision:** the two stale comments in
`.claude/harness/project.conf` and the closing sentence of
`tests/integration/README.md` are corrected to name MT-007. Both are `harness`
and `docs` respectively, writable in PLANNED, and `project.conf` is a Lead PO
path under `rules.md`.

**Consequence deliberately accepted:** between MT-002 and MT-007,
`bash scripts/doctor.sh` reports the `providers` discovery as missing and the
`integration` gate stays waived. That was already true and is now true for a
reason that is written down.

### PO-3 — Orchestrator's independent verification of the spike's central claim

`/advance-story` requires that a subagent's report be verified against a real
command run, and that a claim **an instruction of mine was wrong** be reproduced
independently rather than by re-running the subagent's own probe. The spike made
exactly that claim: dispatch mechanism #2 (*"recent `onnxruntime-gpu` wheels are
supposed to work via the `nvidia-*-cu12` pip packages, sometimes needing an
explicit extra"*) was reported **half wrong** — the extra is necessary but not
sufficient on Windows, and the generation is cu13.

So this was re-derived with **orchestrator-written code sharing nothing with
`spikes/MT-002/*.py`**: a different script, different (seeded random) inputs, a
different timing harness, the **real 1024² detector** rather than the agent's
small conv probe, and four discriminators instead of one. Run twice in separate
processes, because `preload_dlls` is process-global and cannot be undone.

Script: `<scratchpad>/verify_ep.py` (outside the repo — throwaway, not committed).

**Confirmed.** One line of code is the difference between 578 ms and 23 ms on
this machine, and 1.00× is not a slow GPU — it is the CPU. The spike's finding
stands, and my dispatch instruction was wrong in the way it said.

**One refinement I am making to the audit as a result, because `## Decided` is
the section later stories take on trust.** The audit's `## Decided` called the
failure *"silent"*. Measured, it is not quite silent, and the distinction is
load-bearing for how MT-007 detects it:

- `get_available_providers()` **does** lie — it advertises `CUDAExecutionProvider`
  identically in both runs (D1). Anything asserting on that list is worthless.
- `session.get_providers()` is **honest** — it reports `['CPUExecutionProvider']`
  in run 1 and CUDA in run 2 (D2). This is a reliable detector.
- Requesting **CUDA only** does not raise; it silently returns a CPU session
  (D4). So an exception is not available as a signal either.
- ORT logs a loud stderr error naming the missing `cublasLt64_13.dll`, and it
  survives `SessionOptions.log_severity_level = 3` — it is emitted by the
  provider bridge, below that filter.

`## Decided` now says *"fails without raising"* rather than *"silently fails"*,
and names `session.get_providers()` as the check. The audit's own recommendation
— *"MT-007's AC-7 should assert the provider actually selected, never the
provider list requested"* — was already exactly right and is unchanged.

**Also verified independently: the five model hashes in `## Decided`.** These are

## Deferred verifications

Two verifications this spike could not perform. Each is a falsifiable condition
with an owning phase, per `/advance-story`.

**DV-1 — LaMa tiling seams. Owner: MT-019, in its RED phase. WAIVED for MT-002.**

*Falsifiable condition:* inpainting a mask that straddles the boundary between
two 512×512 tiles must not produce a discontinuity at the seam detectable as a
step in mean grey level greater than the within-tile noise.

*Why it is waived here rather than run:* every LaMa measurement in the audit
(E7) is a **single 512×512 tile**. A full page at this resolution needs 4–12
tiles, and building a tiler is production code — out of scope for a
document-only spike under PO-1, and outside the story's timebox. `stack.md`
names "obvious tiling seams" as a disqualifying condition, so this is a real
disqualifier that has **not** been tested, not one that passed. The audit says
so in E7's closing paragraph and calls it "the largest remaining gap in this
spike". MT-019 must not read E7 as clearing it.

**DV-2 — the `preload_dlls` requirement asserted by a test. Owner: MT-007, whose
AC-7 is the integration test that will carry it.**

*Falsifiable condition:* with the `ort.preload_dlls(...)` call removed, MT-007's
AC-7 must **fail**, naming the provider actually selected. If AC-7 still passes,
it is asserting the requested provider list rather than the selected one and is
vacuous — which is precisely the trap the audit's `## Decided` warns about.

*Discharged in part, here, by the orchestrator* — the underlying behaviour was
reproduced with an independent probe (PO-3), which is what makes the condition
above worth writing:

