## Context

**Q1 and Q4 answered by the user on 2026-09-29 (see `## Notes`, "User decisions"). AC-2, AC-3 and AC-8 now state those answers, and they freeze when the story leaves PLANNED.**

Selection pans the canvas but **never zooms** (`docs/wiki/design/components.md` §4.5,
built by MT-016). So a selected region can end up not fully visible: it is larger than
the viewport at the current zoom, or the user pans or zooms it away afterwards. §4.6 is
normative about what happens then:

> an indicator appears: a chevron plus the region's ordinal, pinned to the viewport edge
> nearest the region's centre, drawn in `overlay.bubble.selected` **with the halo**, at
> the same badge size.
> - It appears only for the **selected** region, never for all of them.
> - Clicking it, or pressing `Ctrl+9`, recentres the region.
> - It has an accessible name: "Bubble 4 is off screen. Activate to show it."
> - It disappears the moment the region is fully visible again.
> - There is at most one.

`accessibility.md` backs this in three places: A-06 (the off-screen indicator is stroked
with the halo, like every overlay), A-07 (`Ctrl+9` "bring the selected bubble into
view" is a binding that must never regress) and A-08 (the exact accessible name).
`layout.md` puts it at z-order 4, above badges and below chrome. MT-016 deferred it
(PO-3, PO-5).

**It must be a `QWidget`, not a `QGraphicsItem`.** A-08 requires an accessible name,
and A-13.1 records that graphics items are invisible to UI Automation. So it is a child
widget of `canvas.viewport()`, and two consequences follow that GREEN must not discover
late:

1. *(Corrected at RED, 2026-09-30 — see Notes, PO-4.)* When a child of the viewport is
   entered, the viewport receives **no** `Leave` (Qt sends Leave only to widgets the
   pointer left, and the viewport is still an ancestor), so MT-049's handler does not
   run: the canvas must clear hover itself on the indicator's `Enter` (Contract B6).
2. A `QGraphicsView` scrolls its viewport's child widgets along with the content when it
   scrolls. An indicator that is positioned once and not re-pinned after every scroll
   drifts off its edge. AC-5 is asserted after a pan for this reason.

**Which required gate would fail if this story's artifact broke:** `unit`
(`tests/ui/`, offscreen).

## Acceptance criteria

"Fully visible" means the selected region's mapped bounding rect
(`canvas.mapFromScene(region.bounds()).boundingRect()`) lies **entirely inside the
viewport rect, with no margin**. That is the same predicate MT-016's `pan_to_contain`
uses to return `None` (Q4).

- **AC-1** — Given a selected region that cannot fit in the viewport at the current
  zoom (it is wider or taller than the viewport), when it is selected and the MT-016 pan
  has run, then exactly one `OffscreenIndicator` is visible and it shows that region's
  ordinal.
- **AC-2** — Given a selected region that is fully visible and no indicator shown, when
  the user pans (changes a scroll value), zooms (`set_zoom`) or resizes the window so
  that the region is no longer fully visible, then the indicator becomes visible.
- **AC-3** — Given a visible indicator, when the region becomes fully visible again (by
  a pan back, a zoom, the indicator's own activation, or the selection of a different,
  fully visible region), then the indicator is hidden. It is also hidden when nothing is
  selected, on a page with no regions and on a page that failed to decode.
  *Boundary:* a selected region that is entirely inside the viewport but within 24 px
  of its edge shows **no** indicator.
  *Control:* an implementation that tests visibility against the 24 px inset (the
  *selection margin*) instead of the viewport must fail the boundary case.
- **AC-4** — Given regions other than the selected one lying outside the viewport,
  when the canvas is inspected, then no indicator exists for any of them. Across 20
  consecutive selection changes, the canvas never has more than one `OffscreenIndicator`
  child, whether shown or hidden.
- **AC-5** — Given the indicator is shown, when its geometry is inspected, then it is
  pinned to the viewport edge nearest the selected region's centre, at the position
  along that edge given by the Contract's placement rule. Its chevron points toward the
  region (outward through that edge). This holds for a region beyond each of the four
  edges, and it **still holds after a further pan** that leaves the region off screen.
- **AC-6** — Given the indicator is painted, when its paint calls are captured, then it
  strokes the halo (`overlay.halo`, core width + 2 × `overlay.stroke.halo`, cosmetic)
  **before** the core (`overlay.bubble.selected`), and it draws the region's ordinal. The
  drawn badge is `overlay.badge.size` across.
  *Control:* the MT-016 AC-8 outline checker, or its equivalent, applied to a local
  single-stroke painter must reject it. The test is about the mechanism (A-06), not
  pixels.
- **AC-7** — Given the indicator is shown for ordinal *n*, when the accessibility tree
  is queried (`QAccessible.queryAccessibleInterface`), then the indicator has role
  `Button` and name exactly "Bubble *n* is off screen. Activate to show it.", and the
  name follows the selection when it changes.
- **AC-8** — Given the indicator is shown, when it is clicked, **or** when `Ctrl+9` is
  pressed with focus on the canvas, then the selected region is brought into view
  by centring it; the zoom is reduced **only** if the region does not fit the
  viewport inset by 24 px at the current zoom (to the largest zoom at which it does),
  and is **never increased** (Q1, answered 2026-09-29); the indicator is hidden if the
  region is now fully visible, and `link.state.selected_region_id` is unchanged.
  `Ctrl+9` with nothing selected changes nothing.
- **AC-9** — Given the indicator lies over a region of the art, when it is clicked,
  then no region under it is selected or cycled (the click does not fall through to the
  canvas hit test). When the pointer moves from a hovered region onto the indicator,
  canvas hover is cleared (MT-049), and the indicator itself never becomes a hover
  target.

**B2 — "fully visible" (Q4).** The canvas computes, exactly as `reveal` does,
`region_rect = QRectF(self.mapFromScene(region.bounds()).boundingRect())` and
`view_rect = QRectF(self.viewport().rect())`. `indicator_edge` returns `None` iff
`region_rect.left() >= view_rect.left() and region_rect.top() >= view_rect.top() and
region_rect.right() <= view_rect.right() and region_rect.bottom() <= view_rect.bottom()`
— the predicate `pan_to_contain` uses for its `None`, with **no margin**. (It may call a
shared helper; it must not use the 24 px inset. Deferred 2 breaks exactly that.)

**B3 — the edge.** Let `c = region_rect.center()`. Signed distances: TOP `c.y - top`,
RIGHT `right - c.x`, BOTTOM `bottom - c.y`, LEFT `c.x - left` (all of `view_rect`). The
edge with the smallest distance wins; ties break in the order TOP, RIGHT, BOTTOM, LEFT.
A centre beyond an edge has a negative distance to it, so that edge wins. This is §4.6
read literally: a region that overflows only horizontally but whose centre is nearer the
top edge gets a TOP indicator. That is accepted, not a bug.

**B4 — the indicator's centre** (the `QPointF` `indicator_edge` returns). With
`h = INDICATOR_TARGET_PX / 2 = 14`, `W = view_rect.width()`, `H = view_rect.height()`,
`clamp(v, lo, hi) = min(max(v, lo), hi)` and `view_rect` at the origin:
TOP `(clamp(c.x, h, W - h), h)`; RIGHT `(W - h, clamp(c.y, h, H - h))`;
BOTTOM `(clamp(c.x, h, W - h), H - h)`; LEFT `(h, clamp(c.y, h, H - h))`.
The widget is `INDICATOR_TARGET_PX` × `INDICATOR_TARGET_PX` (fixed size) and is placed
with `move((centre - QPointF(h, h)).toPoint())` — `toPoint` rounds half away from zero.
RED's AC-5 fixtures choose whole-pixel expected centres, or a clamped one, so the
rounding is never what a test turns on.

**B5 — what is drawn (widget coordinates, 28 × 28, centre `m = (14, 14)`).** The outward
unit vector `u` is TOP `(0, -1)`, RIGHT `(1, 0)`, BOTTOM `(0, 1)`, LEFT `(-1, 0)`; `v` is
`u` rotated a quarter turn (`v = (-u.y, u.x)`).
- `chevron_points(edge)` = `(m + 7u + 5v, m + 11u, m + 7u - 5v)` — arm, apex, arm. The
  apex is the point farthest along `u`: "the chevron points outward through the edge".
- `badge_rect(edge)` = the square of side `overlay.badge.size` (18) centred on `m - 3u`
  (shifted 3 px inward so the chevron has room outboard of it).
- `paint_indicator` makes, in this order: `strokePath(chevron, halo pen)` with colour
  `overlay.halo`, width `overlay.stroke.core.selected + 2 × overlay.stroke.halo` (7),
  cosmetic; `strokePath(chevron, core pen)` with `overlay.bubble.selected`, width
  `overlay.stroke.core.selected` (3), cosmetic; then the badge exactly as a **selected**
  marker's badge is drawn (`markers._paint_badge`, SELECTED branch): `drawEllipse(
  badge_rect(edge))` with a halo pen of width `overlay.stroke.halo` and a brush of
  `overlay.bubble.selected`; then `drawText(badge_rect(edge), AlignCenter, str(ordinal))`
  in `color.text.on-accent`. The chevron path is an open polyline through the three
  points (`moveTo`, `lineTo`, `lineTo`). GREEN may factor the badge helper out of
  `markers.py` rather than copy it; it must not change what a marker paints (MT-016 and
  MT-051 tests must stay green).
- `OffscreenIndicator.paintEvent` calls `paint_indicator` with the stored ordinal and
  edge and nothing else, so AC-6 can capture `paint_indicator` on a recording painter.

**B6 — the widget.** A child of `canvas.viewport()`, **not** added to the viewport's
`QVBoxLayout` (which manages `message` only). `Qt.FocusPolicy.NoFocus` (§4.7 keeps three
focus stops; the keyboard path is `Ctrl+9`). Accessible role `QAccessible.Role.Button`
(PySide6's `Button` and `PushButton` are one value — measured). `set_target(ordinal,
edge)` stores both, sets `setAccessibleName(f"Bubble {ordinal} is off screen. Activate
to show it.")` (A-08, exactly) and calls `update()`. The indicator must never let a
mouse event reach the viewport underneath it (AC-9): `WA_NoMousePropagation`, or
accepting every mouse event, is GREEN's choice — **trap:** a widget without mouse
tracking may *ignore* a button-less move, and an ignored move propagates to the
viewport, whose `mouseMoveEvent` would then hover the region under the indicator. The
indicator does not track the mouse and has no hover look of its own.
*Amended by RED (2026-09-29): Context item 1 does not hold. Measured offscreen, a real
`QTest.mouseMove` from the viewport onto a child of it delivers `Enter` to the child and
**nothing** to the viewport (no `Leave`, no `MouseMove`), so MT-049's `viewportEvent`
handler never runs and hover is not cleared. The canvas therefore emits
`regionHovered(None)` itself when the indicator is entered (for example an event filter
the canvas installs on the indicator for `QEvent.Type.Enter`, or the indicator's
`enterEvent` routed to the canvas; GREEN's choice). AC-9 asserts the outcome, not the
mechanism.*

**B7 — re-evaluation.** A private `PageCanvas._update_indicator()` shows the indicator
(after `set_target` and `move`) when the canvas is LOADED, `selected_region_id` names a
region in `self._regions`, and `indicator_edge` is not `None`; otherwise it hides it. It
runs at the end of: `set_link_state`, `set_zoom`, `set_regions`, `set_page`,
`scrollContentsBy` (after `super()`, which is what moves child widgets and so must be
followed by a re-pin — Deferred 4), `resizeEvent` (after `super()`) and
`bring_into_view`. `reveal` needs no explicit call: it moves the scroll bars, which
calls `scrollContentsBy`. The indicator is created **before** `__init__`'s own
`set_page(None)` call.

**B8 — `bring_into_view(region_id)` (Q1).** Unknown `region_id` → nothing at all.
Otherwise, with `z = self.zoom()`, `b = region.bounds()` (scene), `W`, `H` the viewport
size and `M = SELECTION_MARGIN_PX` (24):
1. `fits` = `region_rect.width() <= W - 2M and region_rect.height() <= H - 2M` (B2's
   `region_rect`, i.e. it fits inside the 24 px inset at the current zoom).
2. If `fits`, the zoom is untouched (`set_zoom` is **not** called). Otherwise
   `target = min(z, (W - 2M) / b.width(), (H - 2M) / b.height())` (a zero width or
   height contributes no term) and `set_zoom(target)`, which clamps to `ZOOM_MIN`. The
   zoom is **never increased**.
3. `self.centerOn(b.center())`.
4. `_update_indicator()`.
It never emits `regionClicked`, `regionHovered` or `selectionStepRequested`, and never
touches `_link_state`. RED asserts `m11()`/`m22()` exactly: unchanged (`==`) in the
fits case, `== pytest.approx(target)` otherwise, and the centre of the mapped region
within 1 px of the viewport centre on each axis when the scroll range allows it.

**B9 — the click and the key.** `offscreen_indicator.clicked` → `bring_into_view(
self._link_state.selected_region_id)` when that is not `None`. In `keyPressEvent`,
`event.key() == Qt.Key.Key_9` with `event.modifiers() & ~KeypadModifier ==
ControlModifier` → `bring_into_view(selected)` if something is selected, else nothing;
either way `event.accept()` and **return** — it never reaches `QGraphicsView`.

## Notes

### Open questions (must be answered before PLANNED → RED)

- **Q1 — What do `Ctrl+9` and clicking the indicator do to the zoom?** The design says
  three different things:
  - §3 and the §4.7 table: `Ctrl+9` = "fit selected bubble", listed beside `Ctrl+0`
    "fit page", which is a zoom operation;
  - §4.5: "Zoom is never changed by selection — zoom belongs to the user; `Ctrl+9` is how
    they ask for it", so `Ctrl+9` is a **user zoom request**, not a selection, and it is
    allowed to change the zoom;
  - §4.6: clicking the indicator **or** pressing `Ctrl+9` "recentres the region"; and
    A-07 names the binding "bring the selected bubble into view".

  **The Lead PO's reading of the clause asked about:** `Ctrl+9` *is* permitted to change
  the zoom. It does not contradict "selection never zooms", because it is not a
  selection. The open part is **how far it zooms**, and whether a click on the indicator
  does the same. The options lead to different code:
  - **(a) Recommended — "bring into view, zooming out only if it must".** The click and
    `Ctrl+9` do the same thing. If the region fits in the viewport with the 24 px margin
    at the current zoom, centre it and leave the zoom unchanged. Otherwise, zoom **out**
    to the largest zoom at which it fits with that margin (clamped to 10%), then centre
    it. Never zoom in. This fits A-07's wording and §4.6's "recentres". Like (b), and
    unlike (c), it always makes the indicator go away once it is clicked. Under (c), a
    region that did not fit still does not fit after recentring, so the indicator stays
    after being clicked. Of the two readings that work, (a) changes the zoom least, which
    keeps §4.5's spirit: the user asked, so the zoom may change, but only as far as
    needed. The one exception is a region that does not fit even at 10%, which keeps its
    indicator under (a) and (b) alike.
  - (b) "Fit" in the `fitInView` sense, for both. Zoom so that the region fills the
    viewport with margin, clamped to 10%–800%. A small bubble jumps to 800%.
  - (c) The click only recentres, at the current zoom. `Ctrl+9` fits as in (a) or (b).
    The click and the key then differ, which §4.6 does not suggest.
- **Q4 — Does a region near the page edge trigger the indicator?** The request for this
  story said the indicator shows when the region "sits within the margin of the page
  edge", because MT-016 PO-3 left no scene padding. §4.6 says it shows when the region
  is "not fully visible", and gives "it did not fit" as the reason. A region within 24 px
  of the page edge is entirely inside the viewport after MT-016's pan (the scroll range
  reaches the page edge). It lacks the *margin* but it is **fully visible**, so under
  §4.6 it gets no indicator. AC-3's boundary case encodes that reading. **Recommended:**
  follow §4.6 (no indicator). If the user wants the indicator there too, the predicate
  becomes the 24 px inset, and Deferred 2 flips.
